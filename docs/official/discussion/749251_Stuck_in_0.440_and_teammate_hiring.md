  Similar spot here (0.437–0.438 on the same lineage), so here’s what my own measurements say on your three questions:
  
  - Recall vs ranking vs simulators. Reordering changes transfer to the LB much smaller than they look offline. On my structure-disjoint CV, a LambdaRank ranker with fragmentation features gained +0.14 MRR, while the LB moved +0.03–0.04 for the same steps. The one change that moved the 0.437 public pipeline at all was adding new candidates from an independent engine (my own spectrum→fingerprint MLP + ranker over PubChem/COCONUT), merged by reciprocal-rank fusion over metric identities with the source’s top-1 protected: 0.437 → 0.438. So I’d look at recall/diversity before more re-ranking.
  
  - Structure-disjoint validation. I hold out structures (by InChIKey14) from all training libraries and keep the answer reachable only through PubChem (~5k–10k candidates per molecule). Two splits that behave very differently:
  
  250 enveda-np-examples structures (all have public spectra elsewhere)
  490 enveda-180 structures with no public spectra (class-2-like)
  
  Popularity puts the answer at #1 for 67% of the first split and ~0% of the second, so a library-only CV overrates any prior. For pretrained models I retrain my own with the same holdout excluded; I can’t audit the public FPNet banks the same way.
  
  - Fragments. Yes, they help, but as a relative feature: explained intensity of 1–2 bond cuts (±2 H, 10 ppm), expressed as the gap to the best candidate in the same molecule, was my single most important ranker feature (no-public-spectra split 0.502 → 0.627). Two cautions from my side: your 300 s budget leaving 161/400 molecules unscored would hide most of the gain, and ranking with a different model than the ranker was trained on cost me ~0.02 LB.

    - **Kino Lutor** (2026-10-15, votes 0)
      Thanks for asking. By “fragment explanation,” I mean checking whether fragments derived from a candidate SMILES can explain the observed MS/MS peaks.
      
      The current workflow is:
      
      - Check precursor mass: Calculate the candidate’s exact mass and account for the reported adduct, such as [M+H]+ or [M-H]-.
      
      - Generate fragments:Break non-ring single bonds and calculate fragment compositions while preserving the parent’s atom counts. A separate experimental arm allows two bond cuts and limited hydrogen adjustments.
      
      - Match peaks: Compare predicted fragment-ion masses with observed peaks using a tolerance of max(0.002 Da, 10 ppm). Record fragment formulas, neutral losses, mass errors, and unexplained peaks.
      
      - Score candidates: Measure explained spectrum intensity. A rarity-weighted version gives more credit to peaks that fewer candidates can explain.
      
      Currently, this only reranks same-formula candidate groups without usable ICEBERG/GLACIER evidence. It preserves the existing Top-1 prediction. I also use mismatched same-adduct spectra as a negative control.
      
      These explanations are mass- and graph-consistent hypotheses, not confirmed fragmentation mechanisms. I haven’t established an independent validation gain yet. I’d appreciate suggestions on improving their chemical validity and ability to distinguish isomers.
