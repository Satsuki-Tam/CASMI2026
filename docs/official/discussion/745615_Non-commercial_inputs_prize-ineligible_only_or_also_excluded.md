# Non-commercial inputs: prize-ineligible only, or also excluded from the final leaderboard and medals?
topic 745615 | by **Amin** (https://www.kaggle.com/aminvafaei) | posted 2026-10-03 | votes 1 | comments 1 | https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/745615

Hi David and the Enveda team,
Thank you for the licensing note in the welcome post (741359) and for the NIST ruling (744470). One point is still unclear,
and it affects what teams build for their final selections.
Several public notebooks near the top of the public leaderboard attach datasets whose Kaggle licence is "Other
(non-commercial)" or CC BY-NC-SA, for example mirrors of ChEBI / LIPID MAPS and some pretrained-model and candidate-pool
datasets. The notebook authors point this out themselves.
The Competition-Specific Rules (5.a.3) say a winner "does not need to grant an open source license" for input data or
pretrained models with an incompatible licence. The welcome post says solutions built on data or tools that cannot be
commercially licensed "will not be eligible to win", and that you "reserve the right to disqualify any submission that
does not comply".
Could you clarify:

- Is a solution that uses non-commercial-licensed inputs (and no NIST) only ineligible for prizes, or will it also be
removed from the final private leaderboard and medal ranking?

- If a top-ranked team is prize-ineligible for this reason, does the prize pass to the next prize-eligible team, as in
the Foundational Rules ("Determining Winners", 7.b: "In the event a potential winner is disqualified for any reason,
the Submission that received the next highest score rank will be chosen as the potential winner")?

- How will compliance be checked for teams that do not claim a prize, i.e. medal positions?

- For prize-seeking teams, does the welcome-post guidance take precedence over section 5.a.3? In other words, should
every non-commercial input (data, pretrained weights, candidate databases) be treated as not allowed?
A clear answer now would help everyone plan their final submissions.
Thank you!

## Comments (1 top-level)

- **Marie Killian** **[Organizer]** (2026-10-13, votes 0)
  Open-source datasets with noncommercial licensing provisions are permitted, provided they otherwise comply with the competition rules. A noncommercial license does not automatically make a solution ineligible for prizes or medals.
  
  All submissions must comply with the competition rules, regardless of whether a team is pursuing prizes or medals. Submissions found to be noncompliant may be disqualified and removed from the leaderboard. 
  
  If a potential winner is disqualified, prize eligibility will be determined according to the competition’s Foundational Rules.
