# train.parquet appears to genuinely exclude molecule_id / spectrum_id - can you confirm this is intentional?
topic 742042 | by **Abhishek Jain** (https://www.kaggle.com/vardhmanandorid2015) | posted 2026-09-19 | votes 3 | comments 7 | https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742042

Hi David / team,

Quick schema clarification on train.parquet vs. the Data page description.

The Data page states:

- test.parquet has 12 columns (explicitly listing molecule_id and spectrum_id among them)

- train.parquet has 18 columns, described as "the test columns above, plus" normalized_smiles, inchikey / inchikey14, molecular_formula, ingest_lib, adduct_orig, precursor_error_ppm, num_peaks

That "plus" list is 8 distinct columns once inchikey and inchikey14 are counted separately (they are separate columns in the actual file). So:

- 12 (test) + 8 (train-only) = 20, which doesn't match the stated 18

- 12 − 2 (molecule_id, spectrum_id) + 8 = 18, which matches exactly

And this second calculation is exactly what I see in the real downloaded train.parquet: df.dtypes shows 18 columns, and neither molecule_id nor spectrum_id is among them (both are present in test.parquet as expected).

Could you confirm that molecule_id/spectrum_id are intentionally excluded from train.parquet, and the "plus" wording in the Data page just wasn't updated to say so explicitly? Just want to make sure this isn't a corrupted download on my end before building further pipeline logic around it.

Thanks!

  The way I understand it molecule_id is given so you can pool your SMILES predictions for different spectra belonging to the same molecular structure, because sumission.csvs will be rejected if they contain more than one row for each unique molecule (see quote below). Both molecule_id and spectrum_id are just identifiers for the test spectra and there is no need for them in the train set. According to the  dataset description each test spectrum should have a unique spectrum_id and multiple spectra can have the same molecule_id. The number of unique molecule_ids should be 400.
  
  Quote from competition overview: "Fewer than 25 guesses is allowed but there is no penalty for a wrong guess beyond the rank it occupies. A submission is rejected if it is missing the molecule_id or smiles column, is empty, contains nulls in either column, repeats a molecule_id, or gives more than 25 semicolon-separated guesses for any molecule."

- **Tim** (2026-09-24, votes 0)
  I should say up front that I came to this competition without a mass spectrometry
  background, so treat the framing here as a question rather than a finding. The
  numbers are from my own run, and I am reasonably confident in those. What they mean
  in practice is where I would appreciate a correction.
  
  Frenio's explanation of the missing IDs matches what I ran into. The part I got
  stuck on is what you use in their place, since training spectra also come in groups,
  the same molecule appearing at several collision energies and under different
  adducts. The obvious grouping key is inchikey14. What I did not expect is that it is
  not the key the scorer compares against.
  
  Scoring passes both your SMILES and the answer through RDKit tautomer
  canonicalization before reducing to the InChIKey14, and the inchikey14 column shipped
  in train.parquet is not canonicalized. I canonicalized the 66,490 distinct structures
  across the natural product libraries, and 2,904 of them, about 4.4%, came back with a
  different key. Those 66,490 shipped-distinct structures also collapse to 63,449
  canonically distinct ones, so roughly one structure in 22 has a twin the scorer would
  treat as identical.
  
  If I am reading that correctly, it cuts two ways. Deduplicating 25 candidates on the
  shipped column would spend slots on molecules the scorer already counted, and
  matching retrieved structures against it could miss a correct answer sitting in the
  library under a different tautomer. I have switched to computing the canonical key
  for everything, which is slow but only has to happen once.
  
  My actual question: is this something people working with spectral libraries already
  treat as standard, or is the shipped column usually close enough in practice that it
  does not matter? I may be solving a problem that experienced people here already
  route around without thinking about it.
  
  To your point about the description, train.parquet does have 18 columns with
  neither identifier among them: ingest_lib, normalized_smiles, inchikey, inchikey14,
  molecular_formula, ionization_mode, instrument_type, adduct, adduct_orig,
  precursor_mz, precursor_error_ppm, ms2_mzs, ms2_normalized_intensities, num_peaks,
  base_peak_intensity, collision_energy_ev, collision_energy_orig,
  collision_energy_orig_units.
