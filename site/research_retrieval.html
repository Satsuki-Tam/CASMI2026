Retrieval + re-ranking competitions: methodological lessons for Enveda CASMI 2026 (MRR@25)

Research date: 2026-10-24. Scope: competitions whose core problem is large-scale candidate retrieval followed by re-ranking, scored by rank-based metrics (recall@k, MAP@k, MRR@k, NDCG, mAP@100, F1/F2 over match sets). Primary sources are winners' write-ups (Kaggle discussion/writeups pages, arXiv/overview papers, winners' GitHub repos). Kaggle discussion pages were read through a text-rendering proxy; where a number was only visible in an embedded image it is flagged as unavailable. Competition start/end months given "(approx.)" are from memory and unverified.

Key Question 1: Which retrieval/re-ranking competitions give the clearest lessons, and what did winners do at each pipeline stage? (13 competitions, dense notes)

Takeaway

The clearest transferable lessons come from (a) the Kaggle two-stage recsys competitions OTTO and H&M (candidate generation with explicit recall accounting, GBDT rankers, negative downsampling, time-based validation that tracked LB "almost perfectly"), (b) the matching/retrieval competitions LECR, Foursquare, Shopee and Google Landmark 2019/2021 (union of multiple candidate sources, staged filtering, group-wise features, label-propagation re-ranking with confidence guards, metric-aware post-processing), (c) the LLM-era MAP@k competitions Eedi and LLM Science Exam (retriever recall vs. ranker precision decoupling, cascaded rerankers merged by confidence, explicit handling of "unseen" labels, dedup rules), and (d) the organizer-side evaluations MS MARCO/TREC DL, KDD Cup 2022/2023 and RecSys 2022 (how small the margins among top teams are and how leaderboard noise behaves). Every winning pipeline in this set was two-stage (retrieve → rerank) except the two LECR solutions that found a second stage hurt.

Cited Findings

1. OTTO – Multi-Objective Recommender System (Kaggle, Nov 2022–Jan 2023 approx.)

  • Task/metric: predict next clicks/carts/orders for truncated e-commerce sessions; score = 0.10·R_clicks + 0.30·R_carts + 0.60·R_orders where each R is recall@20; predictions "truncated after the first 20 predictions"; denominator uses min(20, #ground truth) — OTTO overview
  • Candidate space / test size: train 12,899,779 sessions, 1,855,603 unique items, 216,716,096 events; test 1,671,803 sessions, 1,019,357 items, 13,851,293 events; train = 4 weeks, test = following week; overlapping train sessions trimmed — otto-de/recsys-dataset README
  • Local validation recipe published by the host: "create their truncated test sets from the training sessions" with python -m src.testset --train-set train.jsonl --days 2 ...; "If you predict 20 items correctly out of the ground truth labels, you will still score 1.0" — KAGGLE.md. Most top teams followed Radek Osmulski's thread "local validation tracks public LB perfectly" — discussion 364991 (title only retrieved; body not accessible)
  • 1st place (mrkmakr): average of about 1,200 candidates per session (all three targets combined) from (i) visited aids, (ii) multiple co-visitation matrices "with different weighing by type and aggregation period" applied "at multiple times like beam search", (iii) neural nets (MLP/transformer, "no big difference") predicting subsequent aids, some trained only on non-visited aids to avoid overlap with revisit candidates — 1st place writeup
  • 1st place recall ceiling table (local weighted recall@20 of the final ranked top-20, ablating candidate sources): full solution 0.5884 (clicks 0.5566 / carts 0.4364 / orders 0.6696); without co-visitation 0.5831; without NN 0.5831; without visited aid 0.5861; only a single NN 0.5151 — same
  • 1st place ranker: LightGBM LGBMRanker (objective not stated); ~200 features incl. "covisitation rank at candidate generation", "NN cosine similarity at candidate generation", popularity over multiple windows ("It worked well when ranked"), session length, aid duplication rate; ~100 features kept per target by gain importance; negative downsampling kept 5% (clicks) / 25% (carts) / 40% (orders) of negatives "so the training data fit the author's machine (about 35GB per target)"; single ranker LB 0.604, ensemble of 9 LGBMRankers "averaging the predicted scores" LB 0.605; local-LB correlation "almost perfect"; fast iteration by training on 5% of data and evaluating on 10% — same
  • 3rd place (team G&B&D&T, Chris Deotte): candidates from co-visitation matrices, most_common(20)→most_common(50) (50 per user; repo says "100 item candidates (per target per user)"); hundreds of co-visitation matrices computed on GPU, 20 selected by local CV; rules-only with 20 matrices = LB 0.590 (49th place); XGB ranker adds "+0.011" on both CV and LB → single model CV 0.601 / LB 0.601; team ensemble "adding the ranks of each of our predictions per user target type" → private 0.6038 / public 0.6044 — 3rd place writeup; cdeotte/Kaggle-OTTO-Comp
  • 2nd place (ONODERA part): candidates "generated almost entirely by joining the events that have already occurred in the session to the various co-visitation matrices" (teammate silogram, 20–30 co-visit matrices in ~2 min with cuDF); 93 base item2item features (count, time difference, sequence difference, weighted versions, aggregations) expanded to ~5k combinations, "I use just 400~500 features eventually"; XGBoost and CatBoost (CatBoost "slightly better"); team blend "by rank"; "CV/public LB/private LB scores are all aligned" — 2nd place writeup (ONODERA part)
  • Public/private split percentage: not found on the pages retrieved (gap).

2. H&M Personalized Fashion Recommendations (Kaggle, Feb–May 2022 approx.)

  • Task/metric: predict the articles each customer buys in the next 7-day period; MAP@12 with 1/min(m,12) normalisation; "There is never a penalty for using the full 12 predictions"; customers with no purchase are excluded from scoring — H&M overview
  • Candidate space: ~1.3M customers, >100K articles, ~32M transactions (secondary source) — Shaped blog
  • Public/private split: "This leaderboard is calculated with approximately 1% of the test data." (public tab) and "The private leaderboard is calculated with approximately 99% of the test data." — H&M leaderboard
  • 1st place (senkin13 & teammate): 100 candidates per user, deduplicated and merged across strategies; "we don't need to take same N for each strategy, make sure total candidates is 100"; strategies: recent popular items (main), repurchase, ItemCF, word2vec item2item, ProNE user2item embeddings, a logistic regression that narrows the top-1000 popular items to 50–200, graph embedding (~+0.002); candidate recall reported as HitNum@100 per validation week (41,019 / 38,427 / 39,142 positives hit) — 1st place discussion 324070
  • 1st place ranker: LightGBM binary classifier (label = pair bought next week); features = counts over windows, time features, aggregates, differences/ratios, similarity scores from the retrieval strategies (ItemCF, word2vec, ProNE); image/text features "didn't help"; keep all positives and downsample negatives: "1 million ~ 2 million negative samples for each week has better performace"; best single LGBM CV 0.0441 / LB 0.0367; last-week-only LGBM CV 0.0430 / LB 0.0362; ensemble of 5 LightGBM + 7 CatBoost LB 0.0371; CatBoost "much worse than lightgbm"; validation = 6 weeks train, last week validation — same
  • Shake-up data points: 1st place public 0.0372 → private 0.0379 (Japanese Speaker Deck summary, via search snippet) — kuto5046 deck; a 45th/3,006 team reports public 0.0292 → private 0.02996 (via search snippet) — Wp-Zhang repo

3. Learning Equality – Curriculum Recommendations (LECR) (Kaggle, Dec 2022–Mar 2023 approx.)

  • Task/metric: match K-12 topics to content items (29 languages); mean F2 per topic; hidden test "includes an additional 10,000 topics (none present in the training set) and a large number of additional content items"; "The additional content items are only correlated to test set topics"; the source category has no test topics — LECR data page
  • 1st place (aerdem4): pipeline "Candidate Selection (Retriever methods) -> Feature Engineering -> Lightgbm -> Postprocessing"; candidates = outer join of (i) char-4-gram TF-IDF top-20 per topic above 1% cosine, (ii) ArcFace-trained transformer embeddings (3-model ensemble concatenated after L2 norm) top-20 same-language, (iii) second-degree matches (contents of the closest train topic), (iv) rule sources (train topics with same title / same representation text / same parent); ~28 features incl. TF-IDF score and cosine distance "plus max and min by topic_id", is-content-train flag, is-second-degree-match flag; LightGBM binary "hit-or-miss" with minority class overweighted, monotonic constraint and 2x feature contribution on cosine distance; two diverse LightGBMs; post-processing rules relative to the topic's best probability (keep best match; train content if >5% prob and within 25% of best; test content within 5% of best; etc.) — "Postprocessing was very important"; validation = all source topics + random 67% of other topics for training, rest for validation; "improvements in my validation score were almost always correlated with LB"; validation 0.764 vs LB 0.727 then "I got my validation score as my Private LB score, which I didnt expect"; efficiency solution validation 0.718 / public 0.688 / private 0.740 — 1st place discussion 394812; code aerdem4/curriculum-recommendations
  • 2nd place (Konni): single-stage bi-encoder (LaBSE, seq len 96) with symmetric InfoNCE, up to 128 hard negatives per sample, language switching (+0.01–0.02); a cross-encoder second stage was tried and dropped: "if the retrieval model becomes better, it is more likely that the second stage becomes useless or even leads to errors and overfitting"; dynamic similarity margin recomputed each epoch (0.14–0.18; "Using a higher margin leads to better recall by losing precision") worth ~+0.02; 10-fold CV with topic buckets to minimise shared content; fold 0 tracked public LB, fold 2 "closer to the private leaderboard"; example model public 0.65069 → private 0.69607; ensemble of 5 models, 9 min on P100 — 2nd place writeup
  • 4th place (Team Hydrogen): ensemble of ArcFace embedding models only; LightGBM second stage dropped because "while CV looked reasonable, LB was dropping a bit"; threshold "optimize[d] automatically in the kernel to a certain average number of matches per topic"; extra matches added when a topic has fewer than five; new (unseen) content always matched to its top topic above threshold; single holdout splitting out non-source categories gave "strong correlation with this setup to public and private leaderboard in relative terms"; last 3–4 days used "blind fullfits"; blend of 7 topic-based + 2 content-based models — 4th place writeup
  • Private leaderboard ranks 6–10 scored 0.74625–0.74129 — LECR leaderboard. A participant paper reports LaBSE CV 0.66314 — arXiv 2401.09699

4. Shopee – Price Match Guarantee (Kaggle, Mar–May 2021 approx.)

  • Task/metric: group ~70,000 hidden test postings into same-product sets using image + title; mean F1 per posting; "Expect to find roughly 70,000 images in the hidden test set"; "Group sizes were capped at 50"; "Posts always self-match" — Shopee data; 2,426 teams — albumentations listing; "The private leaderboard is calculated with approximately 60% of the test data." — Shopee leaderboard
  • 1st place (lyakaap et al., "From Embeddings to Matches"): main gains came from "properly utilizing image/text embeddings to search matches", not better encoders; public LB ladder: image-only 0.700, text-only 0.640, concat-then-normalize 0.724, "min2" rule 0.743, normalize-then-concat 0.753, full-data training 0.757, union of combined/image/text matches with tuned threshold 0.776, iterative neighborhood blending (INB) + diverse text models 0.784, INB with all three embeddings at stage 1 and joint threshold tuning 0.793; kNN via FAISS inner product, k=51; "min2": always keep at least two matches; INB = query-expansion-like re-embedding (similarity-weighted neighbour sum, re-normalise, re-search) applied in 3 stages; 10 thresholds in total, with stage-2/3 thresholds tuned against the LB: "major score boost came from stage2 and stage3 threshold"; ArcFace with large margins (0.8–1.0 image, 0.6–0.8 text) via gradual margin warm-up; joint image+text training always worse than separate — 1st place discussion 238136

5. Google Landmark Retrieval 2019 (Kaggle/CVPR-W 2019) — 1st place re-ranking paper

  • Task/metric: mAP@100 over an index of landmark images; GLD-v2 "includes over 5 million images"; cleaned train set 1,580,470 images / 81,313 labels (cleaning cut the train set ~2.6x and improved results); Kaggle split: Private 67% / Public 33% — Yokoo et al., arXiv 2003.11211
  • Method: ResNet-101 + GeM, 512-d, ArcFace (margin 0.3); kNN soft-voting (k=3) assigns an instance label to every index image and query; sort-step: "positive samples are moved to the left of the negative samples in the ranking, maintaining the relative order of them"; insert-step: add index images with the query's predicted label that similarity search missed ("we add additional samples from the dataset that were not retrieved by image-similarity"), skipped when the prediction score < τ_score = 0.6 "which guards against queries whose landmark is absent from the train set"; mAP@100 private/public: kNN baseline 30.22/27.81 → +sort 33.79/30.91 → +insert 36.85/34.89 → +αQE 37.34/35.59; "fairly insensitive to the setting of the hyperparameters" — same

6. Google Landmark Recognition + Retrieval 2021 (Kaggle/ICCV-W 2021) — 1st place both tracks

  • Setup: code competition, 12-hour limit on a two-core CPU and a P100; GLDv2c = ~1.5M images, 81,313 classes; GLDv2x = same classes, uncleaned; retrieval index extended with the 2019 index set — Henkel, arXiv 2110.03786
  • Pipeline: 8-model ensemble (3 DOLG-EfficientNet, 3 Hybrid-Swin, 2 EfficientNet), sub-center ArcFace with dynamic margins; per model, rank index by cosine, apply a "soft" version of the 2019 discriminative re-ranking (label query and index images by top-3 train neighbours; if labels match add the index-train similarity, if not subtract 0.1× it), keep top 750 per model, sum scores per image id across models, keep top 100; validation on the 2019 test/index sets with released labels, "very good correlation" to LB; final retrieval mAP@100 private 0.537 / public 0.518 vs best single model 0.478 / 0.464; recognition GAP 0.513 / 0.534 — same; 2nd place retrieval 0.52995 private with landmark-country-aware re-ranking — arXiv 2110.04294

7. Foursquare – Location Matching (Kaggle, Apr–Jul 2022 approx.)

  • Task: match POI records (name/address/coordinates) referring to the same place; "The test set in this competition contained ~600k records", train about twice that — 3rd place writeup; "The private leaderboard is calculated with approximately 75% of the test data."; 1,080 teams; rank 4 private 0.95717 — Foursquare leaderboard; 22,050 submissions from 1,290 participants — Foursquare blog
  • 1st place (re:waiwai / Takoi): stage 1 = 100 candidates per id by lat/lon Euclidean distance + 100 by cosine of bert-base-multilingual name embeddings (cuML kNN); light LightGBM keeps top 20 per method (~40 total); candidate ceiling "Max IOU: 0.979"; stage 2 = LightGBM with ~120 features (Levenshtein, Jaro-Winkler, "per-id max, min, and mean similarity statistics, plus their ratios", distances, SVD of name embeddings), CV 0.875, threshold 0.01 "reduced candidates to about 10%"; stage 3 = CatBoost (CV 0.878) + xlm-roberta-large + mdeberta-v3-base (text + ~70–90 tabular features, FGM+EMA, CV 0.907), weighted ensemble LGB 0.01 / CatBoost 0.32 / xlm-r 0.29 / mdeberta 0.38 → CV 0.911; stage 4 = "merge the matches of two ids if the common id exceeds 50% from either side", rescore new pairs with xlm-r → CV 0.9166; 2-fold CV; suspected leakage from a large CV/LB gap and "LB improving with overfit models"; a leak-exploiting "merge train" step moved public LB 0.900 → 0.943 → 0.971; final private/public 0.976/0.976–0.977/0.978; the team hedged by submitting both (1)+(2) and (1)+(2)+(3) "because they were unsure whether (3) would hold on private data" — 1st place writeup; leak thread "67% of test rows leaked" — discussion 335799
  • 3rd place (Psi): stage 1 ArcFace xlm-roberta-large on concatenated columns, all-pairs similarity cut at a threshold; stage 2 bi-encoder pair classifier trained on in-sample stage-1 results ("I always added all TPs to the training data, and then increasingly added more FPs if time allowed"); blend stage-1 and stage-2 probabilities, threshold; "any random 100k subset scored nearly the same as the full 600k set"; six weeks before the deadline switched to LB-only evaluation (regular feedback, prior CV-LB correlation, full-data training) — 3rd place writeup

8. Amazon KDD Cup 2022 – ESCI Shopping Queries, Task 1 query-product ranking (AIcrowd, 2022)

  • Task/metric: rank up to 40 candidate products per query; NDCG with gains E=1.0, S=0.1, C=0.01, I=0.0; ~1.2M products, ~2M query-product pairs; "the final ranking is based on the score on the private leaderboard" — team www paper, arXiv 2208.02958
  • Winner (team www): cross-encoders (DeBERTa, XLM variants, RemBERT) with "[CLS]query[SEP]color:... brand:... description:...[SEP]", 4-class ESCI cross-entropy (pointwise), ranking score = weighted sum of class probabilities; label smoothing; self-distillation y_new = 0.7·hard + 0.3·soft from 3-fold OOF; pseudo-labelling of public test rows with p>0.7; AWP adversarial training (0.899 → 0.9022 public for the English single model); ensemble weights "set by public scores and local cross-validation scores", correlated models down-weighted; best single 0.9022 public / 0.9015 private → ensemble 0.9057 / 0.9043 (+0.0028 private) — same
  • Final private standings were within 0.0015: #1 www 0.9043, #2 qinpersevere 0.9036, #3 day-day-up 0.9035, #4 GraphMIRAcles 0.9028 (tentative pending code due diligence) — AIcrowd winners announcement; a 9th-place mT5 approach scored 0.9012 public / 0.9007 private — arXiv 2208.06264

9. Amazon KDD Cup 2023 – Multilingual next-product recommendation (AIcrowd; workshop Aug 9, 2023)

  • Data/metric: 3,606,249 train sessions, 15,306,183 interactions, 1,410,675 products; 361,659 test sessions; MRR@100 ("If no relevant item appears in the list, the reciprocal rank is 0"); last week = test, "randomly split into two equal subsets, called phase-1 test and phase-2 test"; Task 2 = domain shift to small locales (ES/FR/IT with 89k–127k train sessions vs ~1M for UK/DE/JP); Popularity baseline MRR@100 0.2875 — Amazon-M2 paper, arXiv 2307.09688
  • Final Task 1 (phase 2) leaderboard: MGTV-REC 0.41208, NVIDIA-Merlin 0.41188, unirec 0.40477, gpt_bot 0.40476, LeaderboardCar 0.40339, AIDA 0.40317 (451 teams shown on challenge header) — AIcrowd Task 1 leaderboard. NVIDIA was announced as winner of all three tasks — NVIDIA blog (conflict with the leaderboard order noted below)
  • NVIDIA method (press-level only): "two-stage pipeline solution ... generating high-quality item candidates and then re-ranking them using engineered features", co-visitation matrices, transformers, multilingual transfer learning, product embedding models — NVIDIA forum AMA; "something like 100 different models all working together to produce a single output"; the team "led comfortably for the first 10 weeks", then "organizers switched to new test datasets and other teams surged ahead", finishing "with a narrow lead"; Onodera: "I really didn't know if our actual scores would match what we were estimating" — NVIDIA blog
  • Accepted solution papers (all two-stage): NVIDIA "Winning Amazon KDD Cup'23", MGTV-REC "A Stacking and Transfer Learning with Diverse Similarity...", unirec "Deep Retrieve then Shallow Rank", YAMALEX "Candidate Reranking Solution using Many Variant Features", AIDA "A Two-stage Ranking Framework" — kddcup23.github.io (PDFs sit behind an OpenReview CAPTCHA; not read). A 10th-place Task 2 paper: candidates by co-visitation, re-ranked by LightGBM with locale-independent features — arXiv 2310.07281

10. RecSys Challenge 2022 (Dressipi fashion sessions; MRR over 100 ranked candidates)

  • Setup: ~1.1M sessions over 18 months; "All teams are required to provide a ranking of 100 items from the candidate items set for each test session"; test 100k sessions randomly split into leaderboard and final halves; "over 300 teams"; final: zzh 0.2160, LAYER 6 0.2148, NVIDIA RAPIDSAI 0.2086, MooreWins 0.2076, THLUO 0.2062 — Layer 6 paper
  • Layer 6 (2nd): validation = last month of the 17-month training period with "a random cut dropping up to 50% of items at session end", which "directly corresponds to the data partitioning for the leaderboard and final sets"; retrain on all 17 months before inference; Transformer (4 layers, dim 128) with masked-session modelling restricted to the monthly candidate set plus a purchased-item objective with 19 sampled negatives; single-model leaderboard MRR: XGBoost 0.1993, VAE 0.2026, DAE 0.2035, Transformer 0.2121; final = linear ensemble of the four — same
  • Overview paper: "The winning team achieved a MRR score of 0.216 which means that the correct target item was on average ranked 5th in the list of predictions" — RecSys Challenge 2022 overview, DOI 10.1145/3523227.3552534; official winners listed as "Zzh, Wei Zhang and Tao Wen" — ACM RecSys challenge winners; Intel "MooreWins" 4th with 0.20762 using heterogeneous graphs + two-stage training + multi-level ensemble — Intel article

11. MS MARCO passage/document leaderboards and TREC Deep Learning (MRR@10 / MRR@100 / NDCG@10)

  • Leaderboard noise analysis (Craswell et al., SIGIR 2021): public leaderboard query set = 5,793 queries; 1,000 bootstrap resamples: on the passage leaderboard the 1st-ranked run stayed 1st in 72.7% of trials, 2nd in 25.4%, 3rd in 1.9%; the 2nd-ranked run was 1st in 24.2%; the 5th-ranked run was 5th in only 34.0%; on the document leaderboard the top run was 1st in 91.2%; "Overall it was very unlikely under bootstrapping that a lower-ranked run would overtake a top-ranked run"; a 45-query private set (TREC 2020) placed the top public run only 4th in expectation (MRR) — arXiv 2105.04021
  • Anti-overfitting policy: at most two runs per group per month and one run with minor changes; "If it were only there by overfitting, we do not think it would be in the top five of forty"; "The best practice for avoiding multiple testing is to avoid reuse of the data"; recommend mean-based aggregation with multiple significance tests (median aggregation dropped test agreement to ~80–85%) — same
  • TREC DL 2019: 43 judged queries per task; rerank subtask re-ranks organizer-provided top-1000 BM25 passages / top-100 Indri documents; best fullrank beat best rerank by only 0.9% (documents) and 3.6% (passages) in NDCG@10 although fullrank raised recall (best NCG@100 +22.9% docs, NCG@1000 +20.7% passages); "we do not observe any evidence that the NCG metric is a good predictor of NDCG@10 performance"; BERT-style rerankers beat best traditional runs by 29.4% (docs) and 37.4% (passages); training labels sparse, "often only one positive label per query" — TREC DL 2019 overview, arXiv 2003.07820

12. Eedi – Mining Misconceptions in Mathematics (Kaggle, Sep 12–Dec 12, 2024; MAP@25)

  • Task/metric: for each (question, incorrect answer) rank 25 of 2,587 misconceptions; MAP@25 with "only one correct label per observation"; "Once a correct label is scored for an observation, repeat predictions of that label earn nothing more"; 1,446 teams / 41,411 submissions; 9-hour CPU/GPU notebook limit, no internet — Eedi overview; "The hidden test set has approximately 1,000 questions." — Eedi data; "The private leaderboard is calculated with approximately 72% of the test data." — Eedi leaderboard; 1,869 train questions, 2,587 misconceptions — Zenn summary (dalab); "many of the misconceptions in the test set were unseen" — Eedi blog
  • 1st place (Raja Biswas, Team MTH 101): retrievers e5-mistral-7b-instruct, bge-en-icl, Qwen2.5-14B fine-tuned with MultipleNegativesRankingLoss (temperature 0.01, LoRA r=64), keeping top 32 plus up to 32 more within a dynamic threshold ("top candidate similarity score − constant") → 32–64 candidates; "Hard negatives and distillation ... improved map@25 but did not improve recall@32"; retriever chosen for recall rather than MAP; encoder-only submission 0.524 public / 0.475 private; cascade of rerankers: 14B pointwise over all candidates → top 8, 32B pointwise → top 5, 72B listwise over top 5 (with three CoT rationales from fine-tuned 7B/14B/32B "reasoners"); final list = positions 1–5 from 72B, 6–8 from 32B, 9–25 from 14B ("cascade merge, not score averaging"); 14B pointwise ablations on private LB: few-shot 0.495→0.531, pseudo-label distillation from 72B teachers →0.575, negatives 16→24 per positive + 2x synthetic →0.596, CoT inputs →0.615; 32B stage →0.625; 72B listwise →0.638; synthetic data: 10.6k generated MCQs covering 4,791 misconceptions (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, curated by GPT-4o); validation 5-fold GroupKFold on ConstructId ("QuestionId was 'overly optimistic' and SubjectId 'overly pessimistic'"), correlation up to ~0.62 with public LB; did not work: iterative hard-negative mining, cross-device negatives, mergekit merging — 1st place discussion 551688; code rbiswasfc/eedi-mining-misconceptions
  • Other top solutions (secondary summary): 2nd = listwise reranking with sliding windows (Qwen2.5-14B/72B, Llama-3.3-70B); 3rd "boosted unseen candidates with a constant so they made up about 75% of the probability mass, which improved their score" after hypothesising that unseen misconceptions dominated the test; 4th "scaled down misconceptions seen in training by 0.4" and built candidates as "top 25 overall plus the top 15 unseen, deduplicated", 47 negatives per positive; 5th extracted 104 candidates and reranked 52 per pass over two passes; the public two-tower notebook dropped 0.482 public → 0.448 private — Zenn summary (dalab)
  • Silver solution recall profile (held-out fold, Qwen2.5-32B retriever): MAP@25 0.4238, Recall@1 0.3017, Recall@10 0.6906, Recall@25 0.8126, Recall@50 0.8978, Recall@100 0.9391; iterative hard-negative mining from the model's own rankings described as "decisive for the medal"; generative listwise reranker (lettered options, gold position shuffled) → private 0.50 — DaoyuanLi2816 repo

13. Kaggle – LLM Science Exam (Jul 11–Oct 10, 2023; MAP@3)

  • Task/metric: 5-option science MCQs generated by GPT-3.5 from Wikipedia; MAP@3; repeated labels earn nothing; 2,664 teams, 57,886 submissions; 9-hour notebook limit, no internet — LLM Science Exam overview; ~4,400 generated questions of which 400 released (200 train/200 visible test) and ~4,000 hidden (secondary Chinese write-up, unverified against Kaggle) — ithelp article; private top-5: H2O LLM Studio 0.933208, Podpall 0.928415, Preferred Scantron 0.927633, Preferred ChattyKids 0.926279, RAG to riches 0.925914 — leaderboard
  • 1st place (H2O LLM Studio: Pfeiffer, Babakhin, Singer): tested ~300 retriever × LLM × wiki-dump combinations; final contexts from e5-base-v2, e5-large-v2, gte-base, gte-large, bge-large over 30M–60M chunk corpora (CirrusSearch dump, 512-char chunks best); science-only filtering gave no significant gain; trained with 3 context chunks, inferred with 5 ("inferring with more seemed to add noise"); answer models = five 7B + one 13B LLM with LoRA and a binary head scoring each option independently ("removes dependence on answer order"), plus mean logits of the other four options as cross-option signal; "A simple average worked best. Max blending ... [was] sometimes better on LB but usually worse on local CV"; validation moved from the 200 provided samples (~0.99 once RAG added) to ~6k public STEM questions; winning kernel private 0.933, single model 0.932; retrieval ≈1/3 of the 9-hour budget — 1st place discussion 446422
  • Cross-team summary: "all teams adopted a RAG approach"; 2nd place stacked retrieval scores, DeBERTa/LLM logits and a "None of above" flag with an XGBoost ranker after observing MAP@3 fall from 0.9 to 0.3 when correct answers were replaced by "None of above" (a safeguard against shake-up that gave little gain because the test had few such items); 5th/7th ran a small model on all samples and routed low-confidence samples to larger models; 4th validated retrieval with zero-shot Llama2-7B; 14th generated its own questions to measure retrieval recall — Zenn summary (yume_neko); a 10th-place team chose two ensembles scoring 0.930 public / 0.923 private while unchosen blends scored 0.924 private — Lizhecheng02 repo

Inferences

  • Across all 13 competitions the "retrieve → rerank" split is universal; what differed was how much of the budget went into each stage. When candidates were cheap and abundant (OTTO ~1,200/session, Foursquare 200/id) winners pushed recall near the ceiling and invested in features; when candidates were expensive to score (Eedi LLM rerankers) winners used funnel cascades (64 → 8 → 5).
  • The ranker stage dominated once the correct answer was inside the candidate list (TREC DL fullrank vs rerank +0.9–3.6%; OTTO rules 0.590 → +0.011 from GBDT; Eedi encoder-only 0.475 → 0.638 with rerankers). This matches the CASMI 2026 observation that 98% of misses are same-formula isomers already present in the window.
  • Ensembling/fusion gains were consistently small (OTTO +0.001 to +0.003; ESCI +0.0028; LLM Science Exam +0.001) compared with candidate/ranker gains (+0.01 to +0.16), except where heterogeneous embedding models were concatenated (Shopee +0.03, GLR 2021 +0.06).
  • The conflict between the AIcrowd Task 1 leaderboard (MGTV-REC 0.41208 ahead of NVIDIA 0.41188) and NVIDIA's "won all three tasks" announcement is unresolved here; the most likely explanation is a post-leaderboard verification or prize-eligibility step, but no source confirmed it.

Gaps

  • Public/private split percentages for OTTO, LECR, LLM Science Exam and Google Landmark 2021 were not found on the retrieved pages.
  • The NVIDIA, MGTV-REC, unirec and YAMALEX KDD Cup 2023 papers are behind an OpenReview CAPTCHA; candidate counts, recall tables and ranker objectives for KDD Cup 2023 are therefore missing.
  • RecSys 2022 winner (zzh) method details were not retrieved; only the score (0.2160) is sourced.
  • Shopee 1st place private score and H&M 1st place discussion-level private score were not retrieved (the H&M 0.0379 private figure is from a secondary slide deck).
  • OTTO leaderboard rows rendered by the proxy (e.g., "NikhilMishra 0.60375") do not match the write-ups' placements and were not used.

Key Question 2: How did winners quantify and raise the candidate recall ceiling, how many candidates did they rerank, and how did they combine sources?

Takeaway

Winners measured the ceiling explicitly (hit counts or recall@K of the candidate set, "Max IoU", oracle recall with ablations per source), then grew it by unioning heterogeneous sources (similarity-based, rule-based, label-propagated, second-degree) and deduplicating, while tuning the per-source quota rather than a single global K; typical rerank depths ranged from 32–64 (LLM rerankers) through 100 (GBDT recsys) to ~1,200 (cheap GBDT with strong features).

Cited Findings

  • OTTO 1st: ~1,200 candidates/session from visited aids + multiple co-visitation matrices + NN predictors; ablation table of local weighted recall@20 quantifies each source's contribution (full 0.5884; no co-visitation 0.5831; no NN 0.5831; single NN only 0.5151); results for fewer than 1,200 candidates "not tested" — 1st place writeup
  • OTTO 3rd: 50–100 candidates per user per target; "hundreds of matrices" computed and 20 selected using local CV; rules-only with 20 matrices LB 0.590 vs 3 matrices LB 0.575 — 3rd place writeup
  • H&M 1st: 100 candidates per customer merged across ~7 strategies with per-strategy N tuned manually ("make sure total candidates is 100"); recall tracked as HitNum@100 per validation week; the retrieval stage aimed at "boosting positive density in each customer's top 100 candidates" and "candidate strategy mattered more than feature engineering" (secondary deck) — 1st place discussion 324070; kuto5046 deck
  • Foursquare 1st: 100 geo + 100 name-embedding candidates per id → "Max IOU: 0.979" ceiling → cheap LightGBM keeps top 20 per method (~40) → stage-2 threshold 0.01 "reduced candidates to about 10%" before transformers — 1st place writeup
  • LECR 1st: candidates = outer join of TF-IDF top-20, embedding top-20, second-degree matches and three rule sources (same title / same text / same parent); source membership and "is second-degree match" become ranker features — 1st place discussion 394812
  • Shopee 1st: union of matches from image, text and concatenated embeddings; "The final union accepts items that either modality strongly suggests, or that both suggest moderately"; iterative neighborhood blending re-embeds queries to recover matches missed by a single pass — 1st place discussion 238136
  • GLR 2019: the insert-step "enables us to retrieve visually dissimilar samples to a query by utilizing the label information of the train set", adding +3.1 (private) mAP@100 on top of sorting, with a τ=0.6 confidence guard — arXiv 2003.11211; GLR 2021: top 750 per model, summed across 8 models, cut to 100 — arXiv 2110.03786
  • Eedi 1st: 32 + up to 32 dynamic-threshold candidates; retriever selected for recall@32 rather than MAP@25 because "Hard negatives and distillation ... improved map@25 but did not improve recall@32" — discussion 551688; silver recall curve Recall@25 0.81 → Recall@50 0.90 → Recall@100 0.94 — silver repo; 4th place candidate list "top 25 overall plus the top 15 unseen, deduplicated"; 5th extracted 104 and reranked 52 per pass — Zenn (dalab)
  • LLM Science Exam 1st: retrieval quality was the main lever ("context retrieval far outperformed their non-context models"); ~300 retriever/LLM/corpus combinations tested; 5 chunks at inference, more "seemed to add noise" — discussion 446422
  • TREC DL 2019: deeper/fuller retrieval raised candidate recall by >20% (NCG) but NDCG@10 by only 0.9–3.6% — arXiv 2003.07820; RecSys 2022 and ESCI fixed the candidate set (100 and ≤40 per query) so the whole contest was re-ranking — Layer 6 paper; arXiv 2208.02958

Inferences

  • The ceiling analysis that transfers to CASMI 2026 is: (1) coverage = share of validation molecules whose InChIKey14 exists anywhere in the union candidate DB (an upper bound on MRR), (2) recall@25 of each engine and of the union, (3) a per-source ablation like mrkmakr's table. Because 98% of misses are same-formula isomers, widening the ppm window mostly adds negatives; the recall lever that matters is adding sources that can produce structures absent from the mass window (generated structures, analog-propagated structures), exactly the role of GLR's insert-step and LECR's second-degree matches.
  • Rerank depth should be set by cost: cheap LightGBM over all window candidates (OTTO-style thousands) is fine; expensive forward-model/spectrum simulation should run on a funnel (e.g., top 50–100 after a cheap stage, as in Foursquare's 10% cut and Eedi's 64 → 8 → 5).
  • Source-membership and within-source rank must survive the union as features (LECR, OTTO) — a candidate that appears in the spectral-library engine and in the analog engine is different evidence from one that appears only in the mass window.

Gaps

  • No competition in this set published a direct comparison of candidate-set size vs. final metric for the same ranker (OTTO 1st explicitly did not test fewer than 1,200).
  • Candidate counts and recall tables for KDD Cup 2023 winners were not retrievable (OpenReview CAPTCHA).

Key Question 3: Which ranker designs and losses won; how were hard negatives and group-wise features used?

Takeaway

GBDT rankers (LightGBM/XGBoost/CatBoost; both lambdarank-style rankers and plain binary classifiers) won the tabular recsys/matching competitions, cross-encoders and fine-tuned LLM rerankers (pointwise and listwise) won text-rich ones, and in every case the decisive ingredients were (i) features normalised within the query group (rank, max/min/mean, ratios to the best candidate), (ii) carefully chosen negatives (downsampled or hard), and (iii) retraining on full data after CV.

Cited Findings

  • Objectives: OTTO 1st used LightGBM LGBMRanker (ranking objective) — writeup; OTTO 3rd "XGB ranker" — writeup; OTTO 2nd XGBoost + CatBoost (CatBoost "slightly better than LGB and XGB") — writeup; H&M 1st LightGBM binary classifier, CatBoost "much worse" — discussion 324070; LECR 1st LightGBM binary with minority class overweighted, a monotonic constraint and 2x contribution on the most important feature (cosine distance) — discussion 394812; Foursquare 1st LightGBM → CatBoost + two transformers consuming text plus 70–90 tabular features — writeup; ESCI winner pointwise 4-class cross-entropy cross-encoders with label smoothing — arXiv 2208.02958; Eedi 1st pointwise "Yes minus No" logit with cross-entropy over groups of 1 positive + negatives, then a 72B listwise ranker over the top 5 (validation MAP@5 0.661, "the best of the rerankers") — discussion 551688; Eedi silver: generative listwise reranker with lettered options and shuffled gold position — silver repo; LLM Science Exam 1st: binary head per option, "removes dependence on answer order"; multiclass heads and TTA over orderings "did not beat the binary model" — discussion 446422
  • Group-wise features: Foursquare 1st "per-id max, min, and mean similarity statistics, plus their ratios" — writeup; LECR 1st TF-IDF score and cosine distance "plus max and min by topic_id", match counts with a mean over topic_id — discussion 394812; OTTO 1st "covisitation rank at candidate generation", popularity "worked well when ranked" — writeup; LLM Science Exam 1st fed "the average next-token logits of the other four options" into each option's head (a cross-candidate feature) — discussion 446422
  • Negatives: OTTO 1st kept 5%/25%/40% of negatives per target — writeup; H&M 1st kept all positives and 1–2M negatives per week — discussion 324070; Foursquare 3rd "always added all TPs ... then increasingly added more FPs if time allowed" and trained stage 2 on in-sample stage-1 outputs (i.e., stage-1 hard negatives) — writeup; LECR 2nd: up to 128 hard negatives per sample chosen by the dynamic margin — writeup; Eedi 1st: raising negatives 16 → 24 per positive plus 2x synthetic data +0.021 private; but "Iterative hard-negative mining" did not work for the retriever — discussion 551688; Eedi silver: iterative mining from the model's own rankings "decisive for the medal" — silver repo; Eedi 4th: 47 negatives per positive — Zenn (dalab)
  • Distillation / pseudo-labels: ESCI self-distillation y = 0.7·hard + 0.3·soft and pseudo-labelling of confident public-test rows — arXiv 2208.02958; Eedi 1st pseudo-label distillation from 72B teachers +0.044 private — discussion 551688
  • Feature selection: OTTO 2nd kept 400–500 of ~5k combination features; OTTO 1st kept ~100 of ~200 per target by gain importance — writeups above
  • Where rerankers failed: LECR 2nd (cross-encoder second stage "useless or even leads to errors and overfitting") and LECR 4th (LightGBM second stage: "while CV looked reasonable, LB was dropping a bit") — Konni writeup; Hydrogen writeup

Inferences

  • For CASMI 2026 the natural training group is "all candidates sharing the query's formula/mass window"; the LambdaRank group should be the query, and each evidence feature (library cosine, analog-propagated similarity, fingerprint agreement, forward-model similarity, fragment explanation ratio, PubChem popularity) should be accompanied by its within-group rank, z-score and gap to the group maximum (Foursquare/LECR pattern). Negative downsampling of easy negatives (far-mass or low-similarity) with all positives kept mirrors OTTO/H&M.
  • The LECR failures show a second stage hurts when its training distribution differs from inference (train topics vs. unseen test topics) and when the first stage is already near-optimal; in CASMI the analogue is a ranker trained on candidates of molecules present in the spectral library but applied to molecules absent from it — validation must include that regime.
  • Listwise LLM reranking of only the top 5 (Eedi) is the model of a "rank-1 polishing" stage: expensive forward-model simulation in CASMI can be reserved for the top few same-formula isomers.

Gaps

  • Explicit lambdarank-vs-binary ablations were not reported by any winner retrieved; OTTO 1st used a ranker and H&M 1st a classifier, with no head-to-head numbers.

Key Question 4: How did winners validate, how much public→private shake-up occurred, and are there documented cases of LB-tuned post-processing hurting on private?

Takeaway

Winners built validation sets that mimicked the test construction (time truncation, group splits on the unseen entity, holding out "non-source" categories), reported CV–LB tracking in relative rather than absolute terms, and retrained on full data; margins at the top were tiny (≤0.002 in OTTO, ESCI, RecSys 2022, KDD Cup 2023) and even 5,793-query MS MARCO flips its top two in ~25% of bootstraps, so a 130-molecule public LB cannot resolve the +0.001–0.02 gains claimed by public notebooks. Documented harms from public-LB tuning are indirect but consistent: second stages or blends that looked better on LB but worse on CV were dropped by winners; teams that tuned to the public set in LECR and Eedi saw systematic public→private shifts.

Cited Findings

  • Time-based mimicry: OTTO host script truncates training sessions to mimic the test — KAGGLE.md; OTTO 1st "almost perfect" local-LB correlation, OTTO 2nd "CV/public LB/private LB scores are all aligned", OTTO 3rd CV 0.601 / LB 0.601 and ensemble public 0.6044 / private 0.6038 — writeups, ONODERA, cdeotte repo; Layer 6 validation with a random end-of-session cut that "directly corresponds to the data partitioning for the leaderboard and final sets" — Layer 6 paper
  • Absolute CV ≠ LB but deltas track: H&M 1st CV 0.0441 vs LB 0.0367 — discussion 324070; OTTO 1st local 0.5884 vs LB 0.604 — writeup; LECR 1st validation 0.764 vs LB 0.727 — discussion 394812
  • Group splits on the unseen entity: Eedi 1st GroupKFold on ConstructId ("QuestionId was 'overly optimistic' and SubjectId 'overly pessimistic'"), correlation up to ~0.62 with public LB — discussion 551688; LECR 4th held out non-source categories so content is "either in training or completely unseen" — Hydrogen writeup; LECR 2nd topic-bucket 10-fold with fold 0 ≈ public and fold 2 ≈ private — Konni writeup; Foursquare 3rd 600k-record holdout with unique POIs; "any random 100k subset scored nearly the same as the full 600k set" — Psi writeup; GLR 2021 validated on the 2019 test set with released labels, "very good correlation" — arXiv 2110.03786
  • Public→private shifts (same model): H&M 1st 0.0372 → 0.0379 with a 1% public split — deck, leaderboard; LECR models 0.65069 → 0.69607 and 0.688 → 0.740 (public < private for everyone, consistent with a composition shift) — Konni, aerdem4; Eedi encoder-only 0.524 → 0.475, public notebook 0.482 → 0.448 (public > private, attributed by top teams to unseen misconceptions) — discussion 551688, Zenn (dalab); LLM Science Exam 10th place 0.930 → 0.923 — repo; Foursquare 1st 0.947 → 0.946 (75% private) — writeup
  • Margins among top teams: KDD Cup 2022 top-4 within 0.0015 NDCG — AIcrowd; KDD Cup 2023 Task 1 top-2 within 0.0002 MRR after "organizers switched to new test datasets and other teams surged ahead" — AIcrowd leaderboard, NVIDIA blog; RecSys 2022 top-2 within 0.0012 — Layer 6; OTTO top-3 within ~0.002 (0.605 vs 0.6038) — writeups above
  • Noise quantification: MS MARCO (5,793 queries) 1st-place stability 72.7%, 2nd-ranked run was 1st in 24.2% of bootstraps; 45-query private set reorders the top — arXiv 2105.04021; TREC DL judged only 43 queries per task yet reported Kendall's τ 0.96–0.99 for collection reusability — arXiv 2003.07820; an AIcrowd simulation of repeated public/private splits found differences "as large as 10%" and that models doing better on public "tend to do worse on the private set and vice versa", largest when class balance differs — AIcrowd thread
  • LB-tuned decisions that winners rejected or hedged: LLM Science Exam 1st kept mean blending because max blending was "sometimes better on LB but usually worse on local CV" — discussion 446422; LECR 4th dropped a LightGBM stage whose LB fell while CV held — Hydrogen writeup; Foursquare 1st kept a conservative variant "because they were unsure whether (3) would hold on private data" — writeup; LLM Science Exam 2nd added a "None of above" safeguard against a possible distribution change — Zenn (yume_neko); counter-example: Shopee 1st tuned stage-2/3 thresholds against the (40%-of-70k-image) public LB and still won — discussion 238136; MS MARCO organisers limit submissions to two per group per month to prevent leaderboard overfitting — arXiv 2105.04021

Inferences

  • Scale of noise for CASMI 2026 (estimate): per-query reciprocal ranks in a 25-slot MRR are bimodal with SD ≈ 0.4–0.45, so the standard error of a 130-molecule public MRR is ≈ 0.035–0.04, and one molecule flipping between rank 1 and absent moves the score by 0.0077. Claimed notebook gains of +0.001 to +0.02 are therefore inside one standard error; even MS MARCO's 5,793 queries could not separate its top two runs reliably. The private set (~270 molecules) halves but does not remove this noise (SE ≈ 0.025).
  • The LECR and Eedi public→private shifts were driven by composition differences (unseen topics/misconceptions), not random sampling; CASMI's analogue is the share of test molecules absent from all databases or absent from spectral libraries. A local validation set should therefore stratify by "in spectral library / in COCONUT-PubChem only / in none" and report MRR per stratum plus a bootstrap CI.
  • Shopee-style threshold tuning on the LB only worked because its public set had ~28,000 postings; with 130 molecules the same practice is indistinguishable from fitting noise.

Gaps

  • No competition in this set documents a quantified private-LB loss caused by a specific LB-tuned post-processing rule; the evidence is winners abandoning such rules when CV disagreed.
  • Public/private percentages for OTTO, LECR and LLM Science Exam were not retrieved, so their public-set sizes are unknown.

Key Question 5: Metric-specific tactics for first-hit metrics (MRR/MAP@k): rank-1 precision vs. depth, dedup of equivalent items, confidence-based reordering across engines, when fusion helps or hurts

Takeaway

For first-hit metrics winners spent their most expensive compute on the top few slots (listwise reranking of the top 5; cascades merged by engine confidence), enforced the metric's guarantees mechanically (always fill slots, never repeat an equivalent item, respect group caps), adjusted scores for "seen vs unseen" candidate populations, and fused engines only when CV showed a gain; blending rarely moved more than 0.001–0.005 unless the engines were genuinely heterogeneous.

Cited Findings

  • Rank-1 polishing: Eedi 1st 72B listwise reranker over only the top 5 was "the best of the rerankers" (+0.013 private) and the final list is a cascade merge (1–5 from 72B, 6–8 from 32B, 9–25 from 14B) rather than score averaging — discussion 551688; Eedi 5th reranked 52 candidates per pass over two passes; 2nd used listwise sliding windows — Zenn (dalab)
  • Dedup rules are written into the metrics: Eedi "repeat predictions of that label earn nothing more" — Eedi overview; LLM Science Exam "that label is no longer considered relevant" — overview; Eedi 4th explicitly deduplicated after unioning "top 25 overall plus the top 15 unseen" — Zenn (dalab); H&M 1st deduplicated and merged candidates across strategies — discussion 324070
  • Slot filling / guarantees: H&M "There is never a penalty for using the full 12 predictions" — overview; Shopee "min2" rule (+0.019 public) exploiting that every item has at least one match, and "Group sizes were capped at 50" — discussion 238136, data; LECR 4th tuned the threshold "to a certain average number of matches per topic" and added matches "when a topic has fewer than five" — Hydrogen writeup; LECR 1st kept candidates "within 25%" / "within 5%" of the topic's best probability — discussion 394812
  • Seen vs unseen population priors: Eedi 3rd boosted unseen candidates to ~75% of the probability mass; 4th multiplied seen-in-train candidates by 0.4 — Zenn (dalab); LECR 4th lowered similarity for content matched to only one training topic and raised it for content absent from training; "New content is always matched to the top-ranked topic above a threshold" — Hydrogen writeup; GLR 2019 skipped label-based insertion when confidence < 0.6 to protect queries absent from the train set — arXiv 2003.11211
  • Confidence-based routing across engines: LLM Science Exam 5th/7th ran a small model on all samples and sent low-confidence samples to larger models; 2nd stacked retrieval scores, DeBERTa/LLM logits and a "None of above" flag with an XGBoost ranker — Zenn (yume_neko); Foursquare 3rd blended stage-1 and stage-2 probabilities before thresholding — Psi writeup
  • Fusion gains and failures: OTTO 1st score-averaging 9 rankers +0.001 (0.604 → 0.605) — writeup; OTTO 3rd rank-sum of 4 members +0.003 (0.601 → 0.6038 private) — repo; ESCI weighted ensemble +0.0028 private with correlated models down-weighted — arXiv 2208.02958; LLM Science Exam 1st ensemble 0.933 vs single 0.932 private — discussion 446422; Shopee "Individual encoder CV rose substantially, but ensemble CV and LB gained less", while modality fusion (normalize-then-concat, union) added ~+0.05 — discussion 238136; GLR 2021 8-model fusion +0.06 private mAP@100 over best single — arXiv 2110.03786; RecSys 2022 Layer 6 linear ensemble of 4 model families (single best 0.2121 → final 0.2148) — Layer 6; LECR 2nd/4th: second stages hurt — Konni, Hydrogen
  • MRR interpretation: an MRR of 0.216 "means that the correct target item was on average ranked 5th" — RecSys 2022 overview

Inferences

  • In MRR@25 the marginal value of slot k is 1/k − 1/(k+1); slots 1–3 carry 1 − 1/4 = 75% of the attainable value if the answer is anywhere in the top 25. Winners' "polish the top-5 with the most expensive model" pattern (Eedi) is the right allocation for CASMI: run expensive forward-model/isomer discrimination only on the top few same-formula candidates.
  • Reciprocal-rank fusion of engines is useful only when engines are decorrelated and comparably strong (Shopee modalities, GLR embedding families); when one engine dominates (a confident spectral-library hit), fusion dilutes it — the Eedi cascade (take the stronger engine's top-k verbatim, then back-fill from weaker engines) and the LLM Science Exam confidence routing are better templates than uniform RRF.
  • Because duplicates by InChIKey14 waste slots, dedup must happen before truncation at 25, and remaining slots should be filled with structurally diverse isomers (different scaffolds/substituent positions) because a second copy of a near-identical structure can only be right if the first was wrong — the same logic as Shopee's "min2" and LECR's "add matches when fewer than five".
  • The seen/unseen prior (Eedi 3rd/4th, LECR 4th) maps directly onto CASMI's "popularity prior" and "in-train/in-library" flags: the optimal multiplier depends on the test population's unknown share of library-absent molecules, so it should be set from a stratified local validation that mimics the organisers' sampling, not from the 130-molecule LB.

Gaps

  • No retrieved source quantifies how diversity among lower slots affects MRR; the diversity argument above is an inference.
  • Exact RRF-vs-weighted-blend comparisons were not published by any winner here (OTTO 1st: "did not test whether this beats voting").

Key Question 6: Checklist of lessons mapped onto the CASMI 2026 pipeline

Takeaway

The checklist below maps each sourced lesson onto the 2026 pipeline (mass-window candidate generation from train + COCONUT + PubChem → LightGBM/LambdaRank re-ranking → RRF of engines → heuristic slot filling → MRR@25 on 130 public / ~270 private molecules).

Cited Findings

  • Candidate accounting (OTTO 1st recall table; Foursquare "Max IOU: 0.979"; Eedi silver recall@K curve; TREC DL "NCG is not a good predictor of NDCG@10") — OTTO, Foursquare, Eedi silver, TREC DL
  • Union of sources with source flags and label propagation (LECR 1st; GLR 2019 insert-step with τ guard; Shopee union) — LECR, GLR 2019, Shopee
  • Group-wise features and negatives (Foursquare, LECR 1st, OTTO 1st, H&M 1st, Eedi 1st) — sources in KQ3
  • Validation that mimics test composition and reports relative deltas (OTTO/Radek, Eedi ConstructId, LECR non-source holdout, MS MARCO bootstrap) — sources in KQ4
  • Metric-aware post-processing (Eedi cascade/dedup; seen-unseen scaling; Shopee min2; LECR slot filling; LLM Science Exam mean-vs-max blending decision) — sources in KQ5

Inferences (the checklist)

  1. Build the recall ledger before touching the ranker: for a held-out set of ≥1,000 training molecules compute (a) DB coverage of the true InChIKey14 in the union candidate set, (b) recall@1/5/25 of each engine and of the union, (c) a per-source ablation table (OTTO style). Report MRR separately for strata "in spectral library", "in COCONUT/PubChem only", "in none".
  2. Do not widen the ppm window to chase recall; it only adds same-formula negatives. Add sources that can supply structures the window cannot (de novo/generative structures, analog-propagated neighbours of confident library hits), and guard label propagation with a confidence threshold as in GLR 2019 (τ = 0.6 there).
  3. Keep every source's membership and within-source rank as ranker features after the union/dedup (LECR 1st "is second-degree match", "is content train"; OTTO 1st "covisitation rank").
  4. Make the LambdaRank group the query; for every evidence feature add within-group rank, z-score, max/min/mean and ratio-to-best (Foursquare 1st, LECR 1st). Keep all positives, downsample easy negatives, and test harder-negative regimes with CV (OTTO 1st 5/25/40%; Eedi 1st 16→24 negatives +0.021).
  5. Use a funnel: cheap LightGBM over all window candidates → expensive forward-model/fragment-explanation scoring only on the top 50–100 → a final "top-5 polishing" stage focused on same-formula isomer discrimination (Eedi 64 → 8 → 5; Foursquare 10% cut).
  6. Train the ranker in the regime it will face: include validation molecules whose spectra are absent from the library and whose structures are absent from the candidate DB, so a second stage does not overfit to library-present molecules (the LECR failure mode).
  7. Fuse engines only when CV shows a gain; prefer confidence-based cascades (take the dominant engine's top-k verbatim, back-fill from weaker engines) over uniform RRF when one engine is much stronger (Eedi cascade merge; LLM Science Exam routing). Expect fusion gains of 0.001–0.005, below LB resolution.
  8. Dedup by InChIKey14 before truncation to 25; fill remaining slots with structurally diverse isomers, never with near-duplicates (Eedi "repeat predictions earn nothing"; Shopee min2; LECR fewer-than-five rule).
  9. Treat the in-train/in-library/PubChem-popularity prior as a single calibrated knob whose value depends on the test population mix (Eedi 3rd ~75% mass to unseen; 4th ×0.4 on seen); set it on the stratified local validation, never on the public LB.
  10. Quantify noise: one public molecule ≈ 0.0077 MRR and the public standard error is ≈ 0.035 (estimate); treat any public delta below ~0.02 as noise, and require a bootstrap CI on the ≥1,000-molecule local set before adopting a change (MS MARCO flips 1st/2nd in 25% of bootstraps with 5,793 queries).
  11. Track CV–LB deltas, not absolute agreement (H&M CV 0.0441 vs LB 0.0367; LECR 0.764 vs 0.727 still "almost always correlated").
  12. Retrain the final ranker on all labelled data after fixing hyperparameters (OTTO, LECR, Layer 6, Eedi all did), and hedge the final two submissions between the aggressive and the conservative post-processing variant (Foursquare 1st).

Gaps

  • The checklist's noise estimate for 130 molecules is derived, not sourced; it assumes per-query RR SD ≈ 0.4–0.45.
  • No competition studied here had both a tiny public set (~130 queries) and an MRR metric, so the shake-up magnitude for CASMI 2026 is extrapolated from MS MARCO bootstraps and Kaggle cases with larger public sets.
