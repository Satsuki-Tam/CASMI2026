# Past Kaggle chemistry / molecular / life-science competitions resembling Enveda CASMI 2026 — winning-solution lessons

Research date: 2026-10-24. Scope: Kaggle competitions whose task structure (chemical-string output, candidate retrieval + reranking, novel-entity generalization, group-wise validation, public/private shake-up) is informative for CASMI 2026 (SMILES from LC-MS/MS, 25 ranked candidates, MRR@25 on tautomer-canonical InChIKey14, ~400 hidden natural products with hidden novelty classes, ~130-molecule public LB).

Method note: Kaggle discussion/write-up pages are JavaScript-rendered; they were read through a text-rendering proxy (r.jina.ai) and are cited by their canonical kaggle.com URLs. Leaderboard "rank change" numbers below are the arrow values shown on the final leaderboard pages (public→private movement); the pages do not label them explicitly, so they are reported as rendered.

## KQ1. Bristol-Myers Squibb Molecular Translation (2021, image → InChI, Levenshtein): decoding chemical strings, validity, beam search, candidate reranking, normalization

### Takeaway
The closest Kaggle analog to "emit a chemical identifier and get scored on exact structure": every gold write-up that could be retrieved converged on (a) autoregressive transformer decoding of InChI, (b) RDKit parse-and-regenerate normalization of outputs, (c) hard validity filtering, and (d) multi-model candidate generation followed by cross-model rescoring, which was the single largest gain (3rd place: single models ~0.8–0.9 LD → 0.54 after reranking). The leaderboard was almost perfectly stable (±1 rank in the top 15) because the test set was ~1.6M deterministic images with 75% private.

### Cited Findings
- Task/metric: "convert old chemical images back to the underlying chemical structure annotated as InChI text"; submissions evaluated on "the mean Levenshtein distance between the InChI strings submitted and the ground truth"; 1st prize $25,000 — [Kaggle competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/c/bms-molecular-translation)
- Scale: 1,171 participants on 874 teams, 10,237 submissions from 64 countries; "146 users competed for the first time, including all three 1st-place winners" — [Leaderboard Finalized / Recap thread](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/discussion/245391)
- Test size: inference ran on "roughly 1.6 million test images" (about 7 hours on dual RTX 8000) — [hengck23 10th-place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/writeups/inchi-1s-c2h6-c1-2-h1-2h3-10th-solution-private-0-)
- Public/private split: private leaderboard uses "about 75% of the test data" — [Final leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/leaderboard)
- Final private standings and shake-up: 1 SIMM DDDC 0.51921 (no change); 2 "∫∫ℳℎ | Zootropolis" 0.53461 (▲1); 3 kyamaro 0.53934 (▼1); 5 All Data Are Ext 0.57197 (▲1); 6 Let the 1024 A100s Bloom 0.57363 (▼1); 9 Translate Chemistry Together 0.61069; 10 InChI=1S/C2H6/c1-2/h1-2H3 (hengck23) 0.61643 (▲1); 11 toxu 0.62773 (▲2); 13 MFL Eindhoven 0.62926 (▼3); ~875 teams — [Final leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/leaderboard)
- 3rd place (KF, lyakaap, Camaro) pipeline = three phases: "Image captioning training", "InChI candidate generation" (several models, beam search), "Candidate reranking" (candidates scored across models). Encoders/decoders: Swin + BERT decoder, TNT + BERT decoder, EfficientNet-v2→ViT + BERT decoder, EfficientNet-B4 + Transformer decoder (416×736 input). Reranking rule: "RDKit's MolFromInchi marks each candidate as valid or invalid"; "Each candidate's training loss (cross-entropy or focal loss) is averaged across models"; "Candidates are sorted by validity first, then by ascending average loss." Effect: "single models scored roughly LB 0.8–0.9, while reranking reached LB 0.54." — [kyamaro 3rd-place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/writeups/kyamaro-3rd-place-solution-brief-summary)
- 3rd place, what failed: YOLOv5 atom/bond detection as input or for reconstruction; MLM pretraining on extra_approved_InChIs.csv; and notably "Pointwise ranking of candidates, using beam-search outputs as negatives for real/fake classification and Levenshtein-distance prediction" did not work — [kyamaro 3rd-place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/writeups/kyamaro-3rd-place-solution-brief-summary)
- 3rd place, train/test shift handling: "The test data had more salt-and-pepper noise than training data. Adding that noise as augmentation narrowed the CV/LB gap"; pseudo-labeling also used; focal loss beat CE; best single model EfficientNet-B4+Transformer ≈ LB 0.74; "bigger is better" for image size — [kyamaro 3rd-place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/writeups/kyamaro-3rd-place-solution-brief-summary)
- 4th place shared a batched beam-search class for a transformer ensemble: candidate scores are "cumulative log-likelihoods divided by sentence length", finished beams are frozen by forcing EOS with log-prob 0, and top-k is selected across all beams × vocabulary — [4th place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/c/bms-molecular-translation/discussion/243787)
- 5th place (CNN encoder + 12–16-layer Transformer decoder, EfficientNet B3/B5/B7, ResNet200D, eca-nfnet-l0; 384/512 px): organizers released "about 10 million InChI strings without images"; the team "used RDKit to render images from InChI for about 1 to 2 million synthetic samples", which "gave little improvement, but it didn't hurt". Noise injection: "some ground-truth characters were randomly replaced with other characters … excluded from the loss, but the model still had to predict the next character correctly after an error", which "made deeper decoders trainable". Invalid-output repair: single-model fallback (~5,000 rows), search of top-12 ensemble candidates (~2,500 rows), and "for the numeric parts of the formula, swap in the second-most-likely digit and then decode the rest (about 500 rows). For example, C12H3 might become C11H3 or C12H4." Public LB: 0.62 raw ensemble → 0.60 after RDKit normalization → 0.57 after fixing ~8,000 of ~14,000 invalid InChIs; "Handling invalid predictions was described as the most important trick for getting below 0.6." — [5th place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/c/bms-molecular-translation/discussion/243943)
- 7th place: predicted InChI with a 193-token vocabulary (max length 280); no beam search ("couldn't get it working"); logit-level ensemble of two ViT-384 + one Swin-384 encoder–decoders (CV 0.71), then RDKit post-processing → LB 0.60 / CV 0.66; normalization labels each InChI as valid / modified / invalid / segmentation_failure; "Invalid predictions were concentrated in very long InChIs"; fine-tuning on InChIs >150 chars improved CV 1.09 → 0.99; invalid outputs replaced via "hard voting" from six other models then majority vote over 11 submissions; label smoothing + cutout fixed failures on long chained molecules and noisy images — [7th place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/c/bms-molecular-translation/discussion/243779)
- 10th place (hengck23): if an InChI is invalid, "the image is randomly shifted and scaled, and inference is rerun … for up to five iterations" (local CV → 0.76); "A final RDKit normalization pass raised the public/private LB to 0.73/0.74"; ensemble via per-row replacement of invalid InChIs from the next-best submission (0.71/0.71); "LB score correlates with the number of invalid InChIs" and the author proposes using that count to estimate LB and extrapolate the ceiling; a separate OCR + MolBuilder + object-detection pipeline reached 0.62/0.61 — [hengck23 10th-place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/writeups/inchi-1s-c2h6-c1-2-h1-2h3-10th-solution-private-0-)
- 2nd place team ("Legoshi's team") was cleared by Kaggle after a multiple-accounts review ("no advantage gained from the multiple accounts"); no 2nd-place write-up is linked in that thread — [Discussion 243811](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/discussion/243811)
- A third-party profile of a SIMM-affiliated (Shanghai Institute of Materia Medica, 2019–2022) competitor describes a "3-phase model featuring ResNet-Transformer image captioning, candidate generation, and multi-model re-ranking" and self-reports 2nd place among 874 teams — [fratch.io profile](https://fratch.io/en/profile/ziming-huang); this conflicts with the final leaderboard, which lists team "SIMM DDDC" 1st — [Final leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/leaderboard)
- Recap thread links to gold/silver write-ups for 3rd–50th place but not 1st or 2nd: 3rd 243932, 4th 243787, 5th 243943, 6th 245704, 7th 243779, 8th 244078, 9th 244031/243761, 10th 243766/243809, 12th 243824, 13th 244166, 15th 243951 — [Recap thread](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/discussion/245391)

### Inferences
- The BMS recipe maps almost one-to-one onto CASMI's database-route + de novo route: (1) generate a candidate pool from several heterogeneous generators (retrieval, beam-searched SMILES decoders, forward-model scoring), (2) apply a hard chemical validity gate (RDKit parse; in CASMI also formula/precursor-mass/adduct consistency), (3) rescore every candidate with every model (teacher-forced NLL or spectral-match score) and rank by the averaged score. This "generate-then-cross-score" reranker beat a trained pointwise reranker in the 3rd-place team's hands, which argues for building the cross-model scorer first and treating a LightGBM reranker as a second-stage refinement validated on isomer-only negatives.
- Canonicalization is not cosmetic: RDKit round-trip normalization alone moved the LB by 0.02–0.03 LD in two independent write-ups. For MRR@25 on InChIKey14, every emitted SMILES should be canonicalized to the tautomer-insensitive key before deduplication so that no slot is wasted on a stereo/tautomer duplicate.
- The 5th-place "formula digit swap" is a direct analog of enumerating neighbouring molecular formulas/adducts within the precursor-mass window when the top formula hypothesis yields no valid candidate.
- BMS had essentially zero shake-up because the test was huge, deterministic and i.i.d.; CASMI's ~130-molecule public LB with hidden novelty classes is the opposite regime, so BMS-style LB-driven iteration is not transferable—only the modeling tricks are.
- The 5th-place finding that 1–2M synthetic rendered images barely helped suggests that synthetic (forward-predicted) spectra may give limited gains unless they close a real coverage gap (e.g., class-2 structures with no spectra), and should be validated class-wise.

### Gaps
- The 1st-place (SIMM DDDC) and 2nd-place write-ups could not be retrieved: they are not linked in the recap thread, the discussion listing renders only partially via the proxy, and neither DuckDuckGo nor Bing indexed them. Their exact architectures, beam widths and reranking rules are therefore unknown here.
- Private scores for the 3rd/5th/7th write-ups are stated only as "LB" values without an explicit public/private label.

## KQ2. Leash Bio BELKA (NeurIPS 2024, DEL binding prediction, out-of-distribution building blocks): chemical generalization, representations, shake-up

### Takeaway
BELKA is the clearest Kaggle demonstration that a public LB dominated by "seen" chemistry says almost nothing about performance on "unseen" chemistry: the winner moved from public rank 850 to private rank 1, and the top-4 private finishers were all ranked ~850–1,023 on public. The winner was a small 4-layer SMILES transformer with two-stage self-supervised pretraining (MLM, then SMILES→ECFP), validated by holding out 3% of building blocks; organizers concluded participants' models "mostly memorize" and 1D-CNNs took 3 of 5 prizes.

### Cited Findings
- Dataset: "roughly 3.6 billion binding measurements covering 133 million small molecules and three protein targets, generated with DNA-encoded chemical library technology" — [NeurIPS 2024 competition page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/competition/84787); the Kaggle release was "~100M molecules" with "a smaller 536k-molecule DEL used as a particularly challenging test set"; the organizers "changed the scoring metric halfway through the competition"; the bind/no-bind classification framing was criticized because the threshold was not disclosed — [Leash Bio, "BELKA unleashed"](https://leashbio.substack.com/p/belka-unleashed)
- Library: molecules from DEL "AMA014" with "a triazine core"; "The library in this competition is composed of 133M small molecules" — [Kaggle overview](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/leash-BELKA/overview)
- Sizes reported by a participant guide: ~98M training examples per protein, ~200K validation, ~360K test molecules per protein; binders ≈0.5%; train file 295,246,830 rows; "the test set contains building blocks that are not in the training set"; metric = average precision per (protein, split group), then averaged — [Zenn guide (Japanese)](https://zenn.dev/yuto_mo/articles/4e417f4ec45f22)
- Organizer slides (text indexed by search): split design where "no building blocks are shared between training set and test set" and "many molecules are lost with building block splits"; the metric was originally "Average Precision over all groups together" and was changed "to be calculated for each (split, protein) group separately and then averaged"; "participant's models mostly memorize" and "performed poorly on molecules that didn't share motifs with the training set"; "models latch onto dataset bias instead of learning to generalize"; "Surprisingly, 1DCNNs won 3 of 5 prizes"; competitors "largely skipped docking, with one group reporting no correlation between docking score and binding" — [NeurIPS 2024 BELKA slide deck (PDF)](https://neurips.cc/media/neurips-2024/Slides/84787_5pLX4pN.pdf)
- Final private leaderboard with public→private movement: 1 Victor Shlepov 0.30619 (from public 850); 2 Smeet159 0.30259 (from 1020); 3 Mohib 0.29028 (from 1023); 4 Takako 0.28772 (from 1004); 5 "mamba1-one-fold-lb0.432" 0.28557 (from 37); 6 mxno 0.28449 (from 799); 7 yaohua tan 0.28083 (from 871); 8 Vlad Vinogradov 0.28059 (from 451); 13 looseRs 0.27789 (down from 11); 14 GORNA 0.27729 (down from 7) — [Final leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/leash-BELKA/leaderboard). Team count 1,982 — [Zenn retrospective](https://zenn.dev/yuto_mo/articles/062b6daff6f1c9)
- 1st place (Victor Shlepov): "a very basic encoder" with 4 layers, 8 heads, key/value dim 32, embedding dim 32 for a 43-token vocabulary; tokenization was "almost character-based" (atomInSmiles applied incorrectly); two-stage pretraining from scratch: (1) MLM with 15% masking (BERT 80/10/10) on train + test + external SMILES, ~100 epochs × 10K steps, batch 2,028; (2) SMILES→ECFP (2048 bits, chirality) with a sigmoid head and frozen embeddings for 20–50 epochs ("Fingerprint MAP was only about 0.4, but the author believes this stage taught useful representations"); fine-tuning head = 3 sigmoid units; BinaryFocalLoss; external data labelled only for sEH so losses were masked; trained on a Colab A100 — [1st place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/leash-BELKA/discussion/519020); corroborated by [Zenn explainer](https://zenn.dev/yuto_mo/articles/13fc8fc77c5147)
- 1st place validation: "held out 3% of building blocks, so the validation set contained molecules with one or more non-shared blocks, about 9 million samples"; "the final leaderboard result was a surprise"; author attributes "much of the result to luck and randomness" — [1st place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/leash-BELKA/discussion/519020)
- 1st place, what did not work: bi-/tri-gram and full atomInSmiles tokenization; depth above 32 or more than 6 encoder layers; multi-input SMILES + fingerprint models; about a month of ZINC pretraining; custom losses; "gated fusion of building blocks" — [1st place write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/leash-BELKA/discussion/519020)
- Participant experience (508/1,982): "there was no reliable CV index"; "evaluated all models only by LB(LB was so shaken)"; could not exploit building-block structure — [Zenn retrospective](https://zenn.dev/yuto_mo/articles/062b6daff6f1c9)
- Community resources: a public GNN baseline reached LB 0.602; a "BELKA split CV like the host" notebook was rated as a strong CV split accounting for the additional core — [Zenn guide](https://zenn.dev/yuto_mo/articles/4e417f4ec45f22)
- Follow-up: Leash released all of BELKA on Polaris (every molecule, replicate, read count, control, building block) to serve as an unrestricted benchmark — [Leash Bio, "BELKA unleashed"](https://leashbio.substack.com/p/belka-unleashed)

### Inferences
- CASMI's hidden novelty classes play the role of BELKA's shared/non-shared building-block groups. BELKA's public LB (which teams could climb to rank 7–37 and then fall to 13–14+ or far lower) is the cautionary case for a ~130-molecule public LB that may over-represent class-1 "library" molecules: a per-class local metric (mirroring BELKA's per-(split, protein) AP) is more informative than the aggregate public score.
- The winning recipe's generalization came from representation learning on *unlabelled* SMILES (MLM + fingerprint prediction) rather than bigger models or hand-engineered multi-input fusion; the analog for CASMI is self-supervised pretraining of the spectrum/structure encoders on the full 2.54M-spectrum corpus plus unlabeled structure databases, validated on scaffold- or building-block-held-out folds.
- Organizers' "models memorize" finding predicts a sharp cliff from class 1 → class 3 in CASMI; de novo (class 3) performance should be measured separately and expected to be low, so slot allocation and ensembling should be tuned per class rather than globally.
- Docking showed no correlation with binding in BELKA; by analogy, physics-only fragmentation scoring should be validated against learned scorers rather than assumed to transfer.

### Gaps
- Write-ups for 2nd–5th place (Smeet159, Mohib, Takako, "mamba1") were not found by domain-filtered or static search; architecture diversity of the prize winners (the slide claim "1DCNNs won 3 of 5 prizes") could not be verified against primary write-ups.
- The slide deck PDF could not be text-extracted locally (no Python/poppler on this machine); its statements above come from search-engine-indexed text of the PDF.
- Exact counts of shared vs. non-shared building-block molecules in public vs. private test, and the official shake-up statistics, were not published in any retrieved source.

## KQ3. CHAMPS (2019), NeurIPS Open Polymer (2025), Novozymes (2022), Stanford Ribonanza (2023), CAFA 5 (2023), Open Problems single-cell (2023), plus Stanford RNA 3D Folding (2025) and OpenVaccine (2020): representation, group-wise CV, shake-up

### Takeaway
Across these, shake-up magnitude tracked the test-set design, not the modeling: deterministic/large/i.i.d. tests (CHAMPS: top-37 identical public/private) were stable, whereas hidden distribution shifts (Polymer Tg shift, Novozymes single-enzyme positional split, Open Problems unseen cell-type×compound, BELKA) produced rank swings of hundreds of places. Winners of the shifted competitions used simple robust models, physics/structure priors, label-consistency post-processing, and validation sets built to mimic the hidden test composition; the only cases where public-LB-fitted calibrations survived were when the organizers' shift was homogeneous (Polymer Tg offset) and even then the report calls it fragile.

### Cited Findings

#### CHAMPS — Predicting Molecular Properties (2019; scalar NMR coupling constants)
- Data: QM9-derived (~134k molecules) with DFT (B3LYP/6-31g(2df,p)) couplings; 65% train / 35% test; test split 29% public / 71% private; 8 coupling types scored; metric = mean over types of log(MAE); ~2,700 teams from 84 countries, 47,800 submissions; prizes $12.5k/$7.5k/$5k/$3k/$2k — [Bratholm et al., PLoS ONE 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253612)
- Shake-up: "The top 37 placings were identical on the public and private leaderboards, because the data was noise-free." — [PLoS ONE 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253612); final LB shows no rank-change arrows in the top 23 (2 Quantum Uncertainty −3.22349; 3 [ka.kr] Solve chem. together −3.19498; 4 Hyperspatial Engineers −3.18085; 2,739 teams) — [Final leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/c/champs-scalar-coupling/leaderboard)
- 1st place (Bosch Research / BCAI; Zico Kolter, Devin Willmott, Shaojie Bai, Jonathan Mailoa, Mordechai Kornbluth): a from-scratch "soft" graph transformer over a meta-graph whose nodes are atoms, atom pairs and triplets (~500 nodes/molecule, embeddings 600–750-d, 14–16 layers); attention penalized by a learnable gamma × squared-distance term (gamma→0 standard transformer, gamma→∞ hard graph mask); separate 2-layer MLP heads for ~33 coupling subtypes; Fourier-encoded distances/angles, OpenBabel partial charges; quad/dihedral features and bond-level Coulomb terms "hurt generalization"; node-cutout regularizer; final ensemble = 13 same-architecture variants, median-based selection of ~9 then averaged medians; best single ≈ −3.08 public, median blend ≈ −3.22, final −3.245 private; code MIT-licensed — [Bosch 1st-place thread](https://www.kaggle.com/c/champs-scalar-coupling/discussion/106575), [GitHub boschresearch/BCAI_kaggle_CHAMPS](https://github.com/boschresearch/BCAI_kaggle_CHAMPS)
- Organizer analysis: all prize winners used encoder (pair features from coordinates/atom types/distances) + feed-forward decoder per coupling type; team #1 added Mulliken charges and valency and "narrowly beat team #2 in the final hours"; team #2 used "a simple Cartesian input with no extra data"; a late entrant (#12) using the rotationally covariant Cormorant network with ~100× fewer parameters was weakly correlated with the top five and received weight 0.149 in the organizers' meta-ensemble; a positive-weight linear meta-ensemble of the top 400 submissions (fit on half the test) beat any single model, with five models weighted >0.11; progress improved ~12× in week one and only ~4× over the remaining 12 weeks — [PLoS ONE 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253612)

#### NeurIPS 2025 Open Polymer Prediction (SMILES → 5 MD-simulated properties, weighted MAE)
- Data: train 7,973 unique SMILES; public LB 295; private 3,207 total (1,354 with ≥1 label); labelled counts train/public/private: Tg 511/95/166, FFV 7,030/86/137, TC 737/239/1,165, Rg 614/54/1,056, Density 613/244/1,282; >10,000 registrations, ~2,600 participants, ~2,000 teams, ~50,000 submissions; ran Jun 16–Sep 15, 2025 — [Open Polymer Challenge post-competition report (arXiv 2512.08896)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08896v1)
- Metric: per-polymer weighted absolute error summed over available properties, averaged; property weights combine inverse training range and inverse-sqrt label count — [arXiv 2512.08896](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08896v1)
- Distribution shift: Tg public mean 102.9 (SD 103.7) vs private mean 179.8 (SD 134.9); the implied linear map (scale 1.3, offset 46) is not a unit conversion; organizers attribute it to differences in simulation/fitting methods; "Two data updates occurred during the competition … The second affected only the private set"; brief release of intermediate data caused leakage detected by SMILES matching; "Calibration that relies on public leaderboard probing is fragile when hidden shifts exist" — [arXiv 2512.08896](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08896v1)

#### Novozymes Enzyme Stability Prediction (2022–23; rank single-point mutants of one enzyme by Tm, Spearman)
- Task/metric: predict thermostability of enzyme variants; Spearman correlation is the evaluation (competition overview) — [Competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/c/novozymes-enzyme-stability-prediction); prizes $12k/$8k/$5k — [Rosetta@home forum](https://boinc.bakerlab.org/forum_thread.php?id=15066)
- Test design discovered by probing: public rows = mutations at residues 16–123 plus wildtype; private rows = residues 124–221 (a positional split of one protein); the split was identified "using only 2 quota submissions, since an all-zero public prediction returns an error that does not count against the quota"; random submissions scored ≈0.215 public — [Chris Deotte, "1st Place Public — Shakedown to 967th Place Private"](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/novozymes-enzyme-stability-prediction/writeups/chris-deotte-1st-place-public-shakedown-to-967th-p)

#### OpenVaccine: COVID-19 mRNA Vaccine Degradation (2020; per-nucleotide degradation, longer private sequences)
- Data: 6,043 RNA constructs of 102–130 nt; top teams "combined natural language processing architectures and data augmentation with predictions from earlier dynamic programming models of RNA secondary structure"; 41% of the winning model's nucleotide-level predictions fell within experimental error; blind predictions on 504–1,588 nt mRNAs beat prior models — [Wayment-Steele et al., arXiv 2110.07531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07531); the final score came from "a private test set of 3,005 sequences of 130 base pairs, whose measurements were made during the challenge and revealed only at the end" — [RNAdegformer paper (PMC9851316)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9851316)

### Inferences
- Shake-up is a property of test design. CHAMPS (large, noise-free, i.i.d.) had zero top-37 movement; Novozymes (one enzyme, positional public/private split), Polymer (private-only data update + Tg shift), BELKA (unseen building blocks) and SCP (unseen cell-type × compound) all produced 200–1,000-place swings. CASMI's hidden classes, Bruker timsTOF instrument, and ~130-molecule public LB put it firmly in the second regime; the ~0.46–0.50 public-LB gains that were "never cross-validated" should be treated as unverified.
- Validation that tracked private results: (i) hold out the *entity type* that is novel in the test (BELKA building blocks; CAFA5 GroupKFold on unique sequences; Polymer Tanimoto>0.99 dedup; SCP stratification by compound), (ii) match the *composition* of the hidden test (GOCurator species-matched 1,000-protein set; Ribonanza length-split sanity checks), (iii) use a *time/future* holdout for stacking (ProtBoost). Validation that failed: public-LB-as-validation (Deotte 1st→967th; RNA 3D 1st place admitted LB-only tuning while a participant reported mass overfitting; OpenVaccine participant), public-LB-fitted blend weights (Novozymes 2nd/Deotte), and LB-probed calibrations (Polymer Tg offset, flagged as fragile by the organizers).
- Robustness under shift came from simple, prior-heavy components: Novozymes' winner was a weighted blend dominated by physics/structure scores (Rosetta ×2 weight) with a buggy GNN; a 50/50 Rosetta+RSASA blend would have medalled; OP3 found simple models beat complex ones; Polymer's winners used GBDT/AutoGluon + fingerprints with heavy curation. For CASMI this supports keeping popularity priors, formula/precursor consistency and library-match scores as strong, low-variance components and letting learned rerankers adjust rather than replace them.
- Length extrapolation in Ribonanza/OpenVaccine is the structural analog of instrument/collision-energy shift: inductive biases designed for the shift (dynamic positional bias; weighted rather than hard filtering of noisy data; test-time pseudo-labelling with variance masking) mattered more than extra features, and an explicit "memorized overlap" rule (zeroing the 13% of test sequences seen in training) was adopted by most teams to avoid rewarding lookup—an argument for scoring class-1 CASMI molecules via direct library matching and reserving model capacity for classes 2–3.
- Label-consistency post-processing (ProtBoost's parent ≥ child propagation; GOCurator's rerank-by-retrieval) is the protein analog of CASMI canonicalization/deduplication plus retrieve-then-rerank, and it delivered the last 0.03 of ProtBoost's ablation (0.59→0.62).
- Best-of-5 TM-score in Stanford RNA 3D is the closest Kaggle precedent to MRR@25's multi-slot structure; the winner filled slots by clustering templates and selecting diverse representatives. For MRR@25 the optimum differs (ranks matter, not just coverage), but the same machinery—dedup to canonical keys, then diversify lower slots across formula/scaffold hypotheses while keeping the highest-confidence candidate at rank 1—follows.
- Ensembling lessons are consistent: equal-weight blends of the top-3 beat each (Ribonanza); 400-model positive-weight meta-ensembles beat any single model and a weakly correlated late entrant received 15% weight (CHAMPS); logit-level or candidate-level fusion across different encoders gave the largest jumps (BMS). Diversity of inductive bias (retrieval vs. generative vs. forward-model scorers) is likely worth more than more seeds of one model.

### Gaps
- Polymer host threads (608103, 608289) and the "Why shakeup?" thread did not render through the proxy; the magnitude of the shake-up is documented only via leaderboard arrows and the arXiv report, and the 1st/2nd leaderboard rows were not rendered.
- Novozymes: the 2nd-place post's "private LB 0.577" and "Private LB was mostly from ~500 rows to ~1600 rows" could not be reconciled with the final leaderboard (2nd = 0.54395); no source documents a rescoring.
- Ribonanza 1st-place private score (rank 1 row) and the exact top-3 architecture table (Supplementary Table S5) were not captured.

- OpenVaccine's confirmed 1st-place write-up was not located (the retrieved thread is a top participant, not the winner); Open Problems 2022 (multimodal) 1st-place summary thread rendered as a user question rather than the write-up.
- No official shake-up statistics (e.g., Spearman public vs private) exist for BELKA, Novozymes, Polymer or SCP in the retrieved sources; Ribonanza (0.82 among top-50) is the only competition with a published number.

### Inferences
- Because no Kaggle precedent exists for MS/MS-to-structure, the most transferable precedents are structural rather than domain-specific: BMS (chemical-string generation, validity, cross-model reranking), BELKA (unseen-chemistry shake-up and building-block CV), CAFA5 (retrieve-then-rerank + learning-to-rank fusion), and Stanford RNA 3D (multi-slot metric). ## KQ5. Cross-cutting: which validation designs predicted private results, which post-processing tricks were real vs. noise, and relevance ratings

### Takeaway
Validation schemes that held out the hidden test's novel entity type (building blocks, sequence groups, scaffolds-by-Tanimoto, compounds) or matched its composition were the ones winners credited; schemes anchored on the public LB produced the largest documented collapses. Post-processing that enforced chemical/ontological consistency (RDKit normalization, validity filtering and replacement, parent-child propagation, dedup/zeroing of memorized items) delivered repeatable gains, whereas LB-fitted offsets and blend weights were fragile.

### Cited Findings
- Validation that worked: BELKA winner held out 3% of building blocks (~9M validation samples) so validation contained non-shared blocks — [BELKA 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/leash-BELKA/discussion/519020); ProtBoost used GroupKFold on unique sequences plus a recent-annotation holdout for stacking — [arXiv 2412.04529](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04529v1); GOCurator built a 1,000-protein validation set matching the test species distribution — [GOCurator](https://www.kaggle.com/c/cafa-5-protein-function-prediction/discussion/466917); Polymer winner removed training monomers with Tanimoto > 0.99 to any validation monomer — [Xebia](https://xebia.com/blog/winning-recipe-for-predicting-polymer-properties/); Ribonanza winner confirmed KFold trends against Hamming-clustered and length-based splits — [Ribonanza 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/stanford-ribonanza-rna-folding/discussion/460121); SCP winner stratified folds on compound and diagnosed hard cell types via fold variance — [SCP 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/open-problems-single-cell-perturbations/writeups/n-jean-kouagou-1st-place-solution-writeup-for-open)
- Validation that failed: public-LB labels recovered by probing + hill-climbing blends → public 1st, private 967th; "Don't select on public LB alone" — [Deotte](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/novozymes-enzyme-stability-prediction/writeups/chris-deotte-1st-place-public-shakedown-to-967th-p); Novozymes winner noted many competitors "had higher private scores yet chose submissions with better public scores" — [Eggplanck](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/novozymes-enzyme-stability-prediction/writeups/eggplanck-1st-place-solution-protein-as-a-graph); BELKA participant: "no reliable CV index … LB was so shaken" — [Zenn](https://zenn.dev/yuto_mo/articles/062b6daff6f1c9); RNA 3D winner used "leaderboard score as the only feedback" while a participant reported "many solutions overfit the public LB" — [RNA 3D 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/stanford-rna-3d-folding/writeups/1st-place-solution), [LinkedIn](https://ua.linkedin.com/in/evgeniimaslov); Polymer organizers: "Calibration that relies on public leaderboard probing is fragile when hidden shifts exist" — [arXiv 2512.08896](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08896v1)
- Metric design against shake-up: Ribonanza organizers selected MAE because it maximized public/private Spearman in rescoring of prior top-10s; achieved 0.82 among top-50 — [PMC10925082](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10925082/); BELKA organizers switched to per-(split, protein) AP mid-competition — [NeurIPS slides](https://neurips.cc/media/neurips-2024/Slides/84787_5pLX4pN.pdf)
- Post-processing with repeatable gains: RDKit normalization (+0.02 LB, 5th place; 0.73/0.74 after normalization, 10th) and invalid-output replacement (0.60→0.57, 5th) — [BMS 5th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/bms-molecular-translation/discussion/243943), [BMS 10th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/writeups/inchi-1s-c2h6-c1-2-h1-2h3-10th-solution-private-0-); validity-first cross-model reranking (0.8–0.9 → 0.54) — [BMS 3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/writeups/kyamaro-3rd-place-solution-brief-summary); ontology min/max propagation inside a +0.03 stacking step — [arXiv 2412.04529](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04529v1); zeroing predictions for the 13% of test sequences present in training — [Ribonanza 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/stanford-ribonanza-rna-folding/discussion/460121); median over 50 randomized-SMILES predictions at inference — [Xebia](https://xebia.com/blog/winning-recipe-for-predicting-polymer-properties/)
- Post-processing that was fragile or failed: LB-probed Tg offset (+0.5644σ) — [arXiv 2512.08896](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08896v1); hill-climbing blend weights on public-fitted targets — [Deotte](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/novozymes-enzyme-stability-prediction/writeups/chris-deotte-1st-place-public-shakedown-to-967th-p); "subtracting the ML ensemble from the main ensemble is risky" — [Novozymes 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/c/novozymes-enzyme-stability-prediction/discussion/376134); trained pointwise reranker on beam negatives did not beat validity + mean loss — [BMS 3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/bms-molecular-translation/writeups/kyamaro-3rd-place-solution-brief-summary); DRfold2 hybrid scored below TBM-only — [RNA 3D 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/stanford-rna-3d-folding/writeups/1st-place-solution); pseudo-labelling helped single models but not the ensemble — [Ribonanza 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/stanford-ribonanza-rna-folding/discussion/460121)
- Ensembling evidence: equal-weight top-3 blend beat each model — [PMC10925082](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10925082/); positive-weight meta-ensemble of 400 submissions beat all, with a weakly correlated late entrant weighted 0.149 — [PLoS ONE 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253612); 13-variant median blend −3.08 → −3.245 — [Bosch](https://www.kaggle.com/c/champs-scalar-coupling/discussion/106575)

- For CASMI's six core challenges: candidate coverage/isomer discrimination → BMS cross-model rescoring + CAFA5 retrieve-then-rerank; de novo generation → BMS decoding tricks (validity gate, noise-injected teacher forcing, length-normalized beam search, formula-neighbour repair) and BELKA's expectation of a steep novelty cliff; distribution shift → Ribonanza/OpenVaccine (biases for extrapolation, weighted sampling, cautious pseudo-labelling) and Polymer (do not calibrate on the public LB); honest validation → BELKA building-block holdout, CAFA5 composition-matched sets, Novozymes' warning; 25-slot ranking → Stanford RNA 3D slot diversification; canonicalization → BMS RDKit round-trip and CAFA5 consistency propagation.

### Gaps
- Ratings are the researcher's synthesis, not sourced numbers.
- No retrieved competition used an MRR-type metric, so empirical evidence on how rank-based multi-slot metrics shake up on ~400-item test sets is unavailable; the closest proxies are best-of-5 TM-score (RNA 3D) and Spearman on single-entity sets (Novozymes).
