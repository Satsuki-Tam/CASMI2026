# Non-Kaggle competitions and adjacent "inverse spectral" problems relevant to Enveda CASMI 2026

Research date: 2026-10-24. Scope: competitions/benchmarks outside Kaggle and adjacent inverse problems (MS→peptide, NMR/IR→structure, LLM elucidation benchmarks, governance analogs, Enveda-adjacent resources). Official CASMI 2012–2022 and MassSpecGym are covered by other researchers and are only mentioned in passing. Each challenge/benchmark carries an assessment on three criteria: (1) domain similarity (MS / small molecules / natural products), (2) core-challenge similarity (inverse spectrum→discrete structure, retrieval+reranking, mass-constrained de novo, novel-entity generalization), (3) usefulness for the 2026 core challenges. Ratings are High / Medium / Low and are my judgments (listed under Inferences).

## Key Question 1 — Which non-Kaggle competitions have addressed spectra-to-structure, metabolite annotation, or related small-molecule inverse problems, and what was learned?

### Takeaway
No platform outside Kaggle (DREAM, Codabench/CodaLab, AIcrowd, DrivenData, Zindi, Signate, Tianchi, Topcoder, EvalAI, Hugging Face, NeurIPS/ECML competition tracks, CAMDA, ASMS/Metabolomics Society) has run a spectrum→structure identification contest for small molecules other than CASMI itself; the closest relatives are two NASA/DrivenData *mass-spectrometry classification* challenges (2022) whose dominant lesson was lab→flight instrument distribution shift, and the DREAM olfaction challenges (2015, 2024, 2025), which are *forward* structure→perception tasks but demonstrate the value of community ensembles and hidden-test governance. The most instructive MS-specific "competition-like" artifacts are benchmarks (MassSpecGym, NovoBench, MolPuzzle, IBM multimodal dataset) published in the NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks track rather than the competition track.

### Cited Findings

#### DREAM Olfaction Prediction Challenge (DREAM / IBM, 2015; Science Feb 2017) — forward problem, small molecules
- 22 teams received the data; 18 submitted individual-perception models and 19 population models. 476 molecules split 338 train / 69 leaderboard / 69 hidden test; 4,884 Dragon (v6) physicochemical descriptors per molecule; 21 perceptual attributes (intensity, pleasantness, 19 semantic descriptors) — [Keller et al., Science 2017 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5455768/)
- Metric: Pearson correlation on hidden-test molecules converted to a Z-score against a randomized baseline. Best individual-perception team GuanLab Z = 34.18; best population team IKW Allstars Z = 8.87; aggregated (ensemble) models Z = 34.02 / 9.17; post-challenge community models Z = 36.45 / 9.92. Best-model correlations: intensity r = 0.56 (individual) / 0.78 (population), pleasantness 0.41 / 0.71, semantic descriptors 0.21 / 0.55. Random forests and regularized linear models outperformed other approaches; linear models were nearly as good as RF — [Keller et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5455768/)
- Organized by Pablo Meyer (IBM T.J. Watson); the winning algorithm on sub-challenge 1 used a random forest; DREAM merges submissions into an aggregate model that is typically stronger than any individual (held-out score 0.83 on 69 molecules) — [Kavli Foundation news](https://kavlifoundation.org/news/crowdsourcing-effort-helps-researchers-predict-how-molecule-will-smell); winner write-up [Li et al. (PMC5824779)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5824779/)

#### DREAM Olfactory Mixtures Prediction Challenge (2024) and Olfactory Mixture Prediction Challenge 2025
- Announced 2024-04-19; task: predict discriminability of odor-mixture pairs from chemical structure; public training data from Bushdid 2014, Snitz 2013, Ravia 2020 (>700 unique mixtures, ~600 pair measurements); hidden test = 46 unpublished pairs — [dreamchallenges.org](https://dreamchallenges.org/olfactory-mixtures-prediction-challenge/)
- Community paper (bioRxiv, posted Dec 2025): 26 teams made 159 leaderboard submissions; 19 teams submitted final predictions; unified training set 168 mono-molecules, 731 mixtures, 507 pairs; test 46 pairs (16 random, 20 manual, 10 "angle"); metrics RMSE and Pearson with 10,000 bootstrap iterations; four-way tie for first (D2Smell, UMich CASI, ChemSenSim Lab, belfaction); ensemble of top-4 + PL21 + Tamarin reached median RMSE 0.08 and Pearson 0.57, beating every individual team and four SOTA baselines; on 50 new validation pairs (measured July 2025) RMSE 0.13; human test–retest r = 0.74; POM-derived semantic features were the most useful; models regress toward mid-range values; generalization to novel molecules "remains untested" — [bioRxiv 10.64898/2025.12.13.694160](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.13.694160v1.full)
- UMich winning pipeline: pretrained single-molecule embeddings (MPNN) → exponential transformation to mixture embeddings → boosting (CatBoost) — [UMich Statistics news, 2024-09-09](https://prod.lsa.umich.edu/stats/news-events/all-news/search-news/olfactory-mixtures-prediction-challenge-winners.html)
- 2025 edition (announced 2025-04-07): predict 51 RATA odor descriptors for >650 mixtures (2/3/5/10 components) built from >145 odorants; panel of ≥15 trained panelists — [dreamchallenges.org 2025](https://dreamchallenges.org/olfactory-mixture-prediction-challenge-2025/)

#### NCI-CPTAC Proteogenomics DREAM Challenge (2017–2018) — the only DREAM challenge on LC-MS/MS data
- Three sub-challenges: impute missing proteomics values, predict protein abundance from mRNA/genetic data, predict phosphoproteomics — [dreamchallenges.org](https://dreamchallenges.org/?p=1024)
- Sub-challenge 1 (imputation) was won by Sunkyu Kim's team (RECOMB 2018 best-performer talk) — [RECOMB 2018](https://recomb.org/recomb2018/timetable/event/dream-cptac-best-performer-1-sunkyu-kim/)
- Post-challenge "DreamAI" ensemble of six imputation methods; preprint claims 15–50% higher correlation than existing tools for less abundant proteins — [bioRxiv 2020.07.21.214205v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.07.21.214205v2.full.pdf)
- No DREAM challenge on metabolomics or small-molecule MS identification was found (searches 2026-10-24) — [search on dreamchallenges.org returned only olfaction and proteogenomics items](https://dreamchallenges.org/?p=1024)

#### DrivenData / NASA "Mars Spectrometry: Detect Evidence for Past Habitability" (EGA-MS; Feb 18 – Apr 18, 2022; $30K)
- Task: multi-label presence of 10 compound classes (basalt, carbonate, chloride, iron oxide, oxalate, oxychlorine, phyllosilicate, silicate, sulfate, sulfide) from temperature × m/z evolved-gas MS; primary metric log-loss (logistic-regression benchmark 0.324), secondary micro-averaged AP; 656 submissions, 446 beat the benchmark — [DrivenData winners post, 2022-06-08](https://blog.drivendata.org/blog/mars-challenge-winners)
- Winners: 1st Dmytro Poplavskiy log-loss 0.092 / micro-AP 0.9483 (spectrogram treated as 2D image fed to CNN/RNN/transformer, no feature engineering, 16× augmentation); 2nd A. David Lander 0.116 / 0.9213 (Conv1d over temperature + linear over first 100 m/z, three normalizations ensembled); 3rd Nikhil Mishra 0.119 / 0.9175 (LightGBM → 31-fold CatBoost meta-model; smoothing gave large gains). All winners ensembled; "domain shift is the central difficulty" (training mostly clean commercial-testbed data, test richer in SAM flight-testbed samples) — [DrivenData winners post](https://blog.drivendata.org/blog/mars-challenge-winners)
- SAM bonus track: best SAM-only micro-AP 0.5186 vs 0.9483 overall; only 12 SAM samples available; winner relied on domain-informed augmentation (random background subtraction, temperature-band sampling, scaling, normalization variants, sample mixing) — [DrivenData winners post](https://blog.drivendata.org/blog/mars-challenge-winners)
- Dates/prize: Feb 18 – Apr 18, 2022; $30K among four teams; run with HeroX for NASA — [NASA STMD page](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/prizes-challenges-crowdsourcing-program/mars-spectrometry-detect-evidence-for-past-habitability/); [HeroX listing](https://www.herox.com/MarsSpectrometry)

#### DrivenData / NASA "Mars Spectrometry 2: Gas Chromatography" (GC-MS; 2022; winners announced 2023-01-12)
- Task: presence of 9 compound classes (aromatic, hydrocarbon, carboxylic acid, N-bearing, Cl-bearing, S-bearing, alcohol, other O-bearing, mineral) from SAM GC-MS; metric aggregated log-loss (+ micro-AP); ~80 competitors, ~500 solutions, 43/80 beat the 0.22 benchmark; winning log-loss 0.14–0.15, AP ≥ 0.79 — [DrivenData GCMS winners post](https://blog.drivendata.org/blog/mars-2-gcms-challenge-winners); leaderboard-top 0.1443 / AP 0.81 — [DrivenData competition 97](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/97/)
- Winners: 1st Nghia NVN (1D CNN-Transformer + 2D CNN "event detection" heads); 2nd Dmitry Konovalov (ensemble of 2D CNNs on 256 m/z × 192 time images, HRNet-w64 backbone, random time-axis resizing because time is only a proxy for temperature, averaging logits not probabilities); 3rd Ioannis Nasios (heterogeneous ensemble; ridge/logistic favored for low overfitting risk under small data + shift); bonus Dean Ninalga (10 MLPs with Label Distribution Learning for suspected label noise; large pretrained CNNs did much worse than small MLPs). Cross-cutting: all used ensembling; distribution shift and tiny data drove regularization — [DrivenData GCMS winners post](https://blog.drivendata.org/blog/mars-2-gcms-challenge-winners); Konovalov write-up — [arXiv 2403.15990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15990)
- Organizers' paper (RAS Techniques and Instruments 2024, 3:156–165, DOI 10.1093/rasti/rzae009): >1,150 unique participants, >600 solutions across the two challenges; motivation = scarcity of representative training data for complex flight instruments — [HAL record](https://hal-insu.archives-ouvertes.fr/insu-04515256v2)
- Follow-on "Stay Curious" (Frontier Development Lab, June–Aug 2024): cleaning SAM Mars data, building an ML-ready dataset combining commercial and flight instrument data, data augmentation for limited data; total prizes across the two challenges $60,000 — [NASA article](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/prizes-challenges-crowdsourcing-program/public-participation-in-machine-learning-bolsters-extraterrestrial-research)
- Related NASA augmentation study (Frontiers Astron. Space Sci., 2025-12-18): expert-validated transformations (whole-spectrum m/z shift ±1.5, m/z "stretch" for calibration non-linearity, 0.7–1.3× intensity rescaling of 30% of peaks, low-level noise) on ~30,000 LDMS spectra from the MOMA engineering test unit; macro recall >0.95 at high augmentation; Pareto region 50–70% augmentation — [Da Poian et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2025.1706125/abstract)

### Inferences
- Assessment — DREAM Olfaction (2015/2024/2025): (1) Domain Medium (small molecules, no MS); (2) Core challenge Low (forward problem, continuous targets); (3) Usefulness Medium: governance template (hidden 69-molecule test, Z-score against randomized baseline, post-challenge "community ensemble" that beat every team in both 2015 and 2024) and the recurring finding that pretrained molecular embeddings + gradient boosting win on small-data molecular tasks.
- Assessment — NCI-CPTAC Proteogenomics DREAM: (1) Medium (LC-MS/MS data, proteins); (2) Low; (3) Low–Medium: again an ensemble-of-six beat individual methods.
- Assessment — Mars Spectrometry 1 & 2: (1) High on instrument (mass spectra), Low on analyte (inorganic/organic classes, not structures); (2) Low–Medium (classification, not structure); (3) High for the *instrument/distribution-shift* core challenge: all winners found that test data from the flight-like instrument (SAM) was the bottleneck (SAM micro-AP 0.52 vs 0.95 overall), and the only remedies that worked were heavy physics-plausible augmentation, ensembling across preprocessing/normalizations, folds and model families, and deliberately low-variance models. This maps directly onto the 2026 shift from 11 mixed-instrument public libraries to Bruker timsTOF test spectra: treat Enveda-180 (timsTOF) as the "SAM-like" in-domain anchor, augment m/z calibration offsets/intensity scalings, and ensemble across spectral normalizations.
- Assessment — Learning to Smell: (1) Medium; (2) Low; (3) Low–Medium: its best-of-5 Jaccard metric is a precedent for "submit several guesses, be scored on the best" — the same logic as MRR@25, i.e., diversity across the 25 slots pays when labels are ambiguous.
- Platform conclusion: the 2026 Kaggle contest is effectively the first MS→structure contest on a mainstream ML platform; prior "competition" knowledge for this exact task lives in CASMI (covered elsewhere) and in benchmark papers rather than contest write-ups.

## Key Question 2 — Proteomics de novo peptide sequencing from MS/MS as a structural analog: benchmarks, methods, evaluation, transferable techniques

### Takeaway
De novo peptide sequencing is the closest mature analog of "spectrum → discrete structure under a precursor-mass constraint": transformers trained on ~30M labeled spectra (Casanovo, InstaNovo), precursor-mass-constrained decoding (beam search + ppm filter, knapsack filtering, π-PrimeNovo's DP "precursor mass control" worth ~+7% peptide recall), diffusion-based iterative refinement (InstaNovo+, +33–42% PSMs over Casanovo), and hybrid database-scoring (Casanovo-DB) are all directly transferable patterns; the field's hard-won evaluation lessons (species-disjoint splits, repaired "nine-species" benchmark, isobaric I/L tolerance, mass-tolerant residue matching, database-search-derived labels as imperfect ground truth) mirror the 2026 contest's identity-disjoint validation and isomer-discrimination problems.

### Cited Findings

#### Benchmarks
- DeepNovo (PNAS 2017; Tran, Zhang, Xin, Shan, Li) introduced the CNN+LSTM de novo model and the multi-species benchmark; reported 7.7–22.9% higher amino-acid-level and 38.1–64.0% higher peptide-level accuracy than prior tools — [Papers with Code entry for the PNAS paper](https://astro.paperswithcode.com/paper/de-novo-peptide-sequencing-by-deep-learning) (aggregator; flag)
- NovoBench (arXiv 2406.11906; NeurIPS 2024 D&B; Westlake) standardizes three datasets with species-held-out test sets: Seven-species 317,009/17,740/17,094 (low-res), Nine-species 499,402/28,572/27,142 (high-res, from the DeepNovo set MSV000081382, yeast held out), HC-PT 213,284/25,718/26,536 (synthetic human peptides incl. HLA) — [NovoBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11906v2)
- Revised "balanced" nine-species benchmark (Wen & Noble, Scientific Data 11:1207, 2024-11-08, DOI 10.1038/s41597-024-04068-4, PMC11549408) — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22multi-species%20benchmark%20for%20training%20and%20validating%20mass%20spectrometry%20proteomics%22&format=json&resultType=lite); Noble's account of rebuilding it: ten versions; fixes for peptides shared across species (leakage), isotope-error mis-annotations, missing N-terminal modifications (Tide bug), repeated scan numbers, modification sets inconsistent with MassIVE-KB training, Param-Medic-estimated vs publication parameters; ground truth = Tide+Percolator at 1% PSM FDR — [Noble, "Wrangling a de novo sequencing benchmark", 2024-11-12](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/wrangling-a-de-novo-sequencing-benchmark)
- ProteoBench "De Novo – DDA – HCD" live module uses that balanced benchmark (779,879 PSMs after downsampling; Orbitrap; shared peptides removed); metrics: mass-based residue match (DeepNovo convention) or exact match where only I/L substitutions are tolerated; peptide precision = correct / predictions above threshold, recall = correct / all spectra (coverage); stratified analyses by PTM, missing complementary ions, length, species; asks submitters to disclose any retraining on test data; accepted tools AdaNovo, Casanovo, PepNet, π-HelixNovo, π-PrimeNovo (InstaNovo "coming soon") — [ProteoBench docs v0.15.3](https://proteobench.readthedocs.io/en/0.15.3/available-modules/active-modules/11-denovo-dda-hcd/)
- Amino-acid precision convention: a predicted residue counts as correct if its mass is within 0.1 Da of the ground-truth residue (DeepNovo convention) — [arXiv 2510.08169 (CrossNovo)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08169); Casanovo adds that the prefix or suffix mass must also be within 0.5 Da and a peptide is correct only if all residues match — [Casanovo, Nat Commun 2024 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11289372/)
- Isobaric ambiguity: leucine and isoleucine are "practically indistinguishable" by conventional MS/MS; distinguishing them historically required side-chain (w-ion) fragmentation or data-driven models — [UWO thesis](https://ir.lib.uwo.ca/etd/7172)

#### Methods and reported numbers
- Casanovo (Yilmaz, Fondrie, Bittremieux et al., Nature Communications 2024): encoder–decoder transformer (9 layers, d=512, ~47M params); sinusoidal m/z encoding, learned intensity encoding; trained on MassIVE-KB 30,506,973 PSMs (~28M/1M/1M peptide-disjoint split); diminishing returns after ~1M PSMs; beam search terminates when mass reaches/exceeds precursor; final output = highest-scoring beam within the precursor ppm tolerance (ε set to the ground-truth database-search tolerance), with non-matching PSMs ranked last rather than dropped; nine-species (original, cross-validated) peptide-level AP 0.81 vs Novor 0.58, DeepNovo 0.70, PointNovo 0.74; on the revised benchmark AP 0.83→0.95 when trained on MassIVE-KB, predictions within 30 ppm 70%→97%; AA-level AP 0.98; HLA immunopeptidomics: non-enzymatic fine-tune raised AP 0.60→0.83 and 87% of Casanovo-only peptides were predicted MHC binders; the paper does not score I/L specially (distinct tokens); authors note labels come from Tide+Percolator and that "no rigorous confidence estimation or FDR control" exists for de novo — [Casanovo (PMC11289372)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11289372/)
- Casanovo 5.0 (bioRxiv 2025-07-25): peptide score = product (not mean) of residue scores → calibration median gap 0.028 with AP unchanged (80.86% vs 80.87%); experimental `db_search` mode scoring database peptides with the learned model: +61.3% (human), +57.1% (mouse), +82.3% (yeast) peptide detections vs Tide XCorr at 1% FDR but ~264× slower than Tide; beam search stops when mass exceeds precursor + tolerance; training 11× / inference 3.5× faster than v4.3 — [Improvements to Casanovo](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.25.666826v1.full)
- Casanovo-DB (learned score function for database search; Bioinformatics 2024): 31–40% more peptides than XCorr, 52–69% more than Hyperscore, 88–102% more than Andromeda at 1% FDR on E. coli/yeast/human; Percolator re-scoring helps it more than classic scorers — [PMC11211853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11211853/)
- InstaNovo / InstaNovo+ (Eloff et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 7(4):565–579, March 2025, DOI 10.1038/s42256-025-01019-5): ~95M-param transformer (9+9 layers, 16 heads, d=768); trained on ProteomeTools HC-PT 2.6M (one PSM per peptide) and AC-PT 28M spectra, 742k unique peptides, 80:10:10 peptide-disjoint; InstaNovo+ = same encoder + multinomial discrete diffusion decoder (20 steps, 5 samples, choose sample matching precursor mass with highest log-prob, fall back to InstaNovo); knapsack beam search keeps only sequences within 50 ppm of precursor — recall drops only 0.05–0.2% while error rates fall; yeast at 5% FDR: Casanovo 39,659 PSMs, IN 39,830, IN+ 52,633 (+32.71%), IN+IN+ 56,230 (+41.78%); 8 external datasets without fine-tuning: peptide accuracy IN 48.3% ± 19.4, IN+ 51.5% ± 21.1; ~34% of PSMs novel vs database search; immunopeptidomics +41.53% identifications; authors: "scale is the most determining factor"; database-search PSMs as ground truth acknowledged as flawed — [Europe PMC full text (PMC7618892)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7618892/fullTextXML); DOI — [10.1038/s42256-025-01019-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01019-5)
- π-PrimeNovo (Zhang et al., Nature Communications, Jan 2025): non-autoregressive transformer with "precursor mass control" (PMC) decoding framed as a knapsack-like DP over mass bins (CTC blank rules), CUDA-accelerated; nine-species average peptide recall 64% vs Casanovo V2 54% vs Casanovo 45%; revised nine-species 75% vs 65%; ablation: NAT alone ≈ +2%, PMC ≈ +7% more; 28× faster than Casanovo V2 with beam search (89× without PMC) — [PMC11695716](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11695716/)
- GraphNovo (Nature Machine Intelligence 2023): two-stage GNN — path discovery over the spectrum graph, then sequence completion — to stop error accumulation in regions of missing fragmentation — [DOI 10.1038/s42256-023-00738-x](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/s42256-023-00738-x)
- π-HelixNovo (Briefings in Bioinformatics 2024): adds a "complementary spectrum" (precursor-mass-reflected peaks) to recover missing ions — [DOI 10.1093/bib/bbae021](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbae021)
- NovoBench head-to-head (AA precision/recall; peptide precision/AUC): Nine-species — DeepNovo 0.696/0.638; 0.428/0.376; PointNovo 0.740/0.671; 0.480/0.436; Casanovo 0.697/0.696; 0.481/0.439; InstaNovo 0.736/0.703; 0.532/0.495; AdaNovo 0.698/0.709; 0.505/0.469; π-HelixNovo 0.765/0.758; 0.517/0.453. HC-PT — InstaNovo best (AA 0.714/0.712; peptide 0.572/0.549). Robustness: precision falls with peptide length and sharply with missing-fragmentation ratio (>50% of nine-species spectra have MFR in [0,0.1)); PTM identification consistently weaker; traditional models remain competitive; prior papers used different subsets under the same dataset names — [NovoBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11906v2)
- Later SOTA on 9-Species-v2: CrossNovo average AA precision 0.906, peptide recall 0.786 — [arXiv 2510.08169](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08169); pairwise mass-difference attention adds 7.4% peptide precision over Casanovo on the original benchmark (J. Proteome Res. 2025) — [TUM record](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/pairwise-attention-leveraging-mass-differences-to-enhance-de-novo/)
- DiffuNovo (arXiv 2602.20209, 2026) argues precursor mass is "either treated as just another numerical input feature or used merely as a post-processing filter" and proposes regressor-guided diffusion with explicit peptide-level mass control — [arXiv 2602.20209](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20209)

### Inferences
- Assessment — de novo peptide sequencing (field): (1) Domain Medium–High (MS/MS, but peptides not small molecules); (2) Core challenge High (sequence-to-structure under precursor mass; isobaric ambiguity I/L ≈ same-formula isomers; novel-entity generalization via species-held-out splits; database-assisted vs de novo hybrid); (3) Usefulness High.
- Transferable technique 1 — mass-constrained decoding: every strong system enforces the precursor constraint (Casanovo ppm filter over beams; InstaNovo knapsack beam at 50 ppm; π-PrimeNovo DP, worth ~+7% recall). For small molecules the analog is formula/precursor-mass-constrained SMILES/graph decoding plus post-hoc exact-formula filtering of all 25 slots; the DiffuNovo critique suggests building the constraint into the generator rather than only filtering.
- Transferable technique 2 — hybrid retrieval + learned scoring: Casanovo-DB and Casanovo 5.0 show a generative model's likelihood is an excellent *reranker* of database candidates (+31–102% detections at fixed FDR). For 2026 this supports using a de novo model's log-likelihood (or a spectrum-conditioned scorer) to rerank PubChem/COCONUT same-formula isomers — the 98%-of-errors case.
- Transferable technique 3 — iterative refinement/diffusion: InstaNovo+ gains +33–42% PSMs by refining autoregressive drafts; an analogous "draft then refine under mass constraint" loop (e.g., generator → graph edits that preserve formula) is plausible for de novo slots.
- Transferable technique 4 — data scale and quality: Casanovo found diminishing returns beyond ~1M PSMs but a 0.83→0.95 AP jump from cleaner/larger training data; InstaNovo calls scale the main driver. 2.54M spectra for 276k structures is in the regime where label quality (library curation, adduct correctness) matters as much as count.
- Transferable evaluation lessons: (a) hold out *entities* (species/peptides) not spectra; (b) remove entities shared across train/test (Noble's shared-peptide fix ≈ InChIKey14/tautomer de-duplication); (c) tolerate isobaric swaps explicitly in the metric (ProteoBench I/L rule ≈ the contest's tautomer-canonical InChIKey14); (d) report precision at coverage (PR curves) — a confidence model that abstains/reorders is what turns raw accuracy into MRR; (e) labels derived from database search are themselves noisy — analogous to library annotations in GNPS/MassBank.
- Isobaric ambiguity analog: I/L (and GG≈N-type compositions) are unresolvable from backbone fragments alone; proteomics "solves" it by metric tolerance or orthogonal evidence. For same-formula isomers in natural products, orthogonal signals (CCS/ion mobility from timsTOF, retention behavior, taxonomy priors from COCONUT) are the equivalent of w-ions.

### Gaps
- Casanovo and InstaNovo papers do not state an explicit I/L scoring rule; only ProteoBench documents the I/L tolerance in "exact" mode.
- Per-species precision tables for Casanovo/InstaNovo were truncated in fetched text; numbers above are dataset-level.
- No published study was found that transfers peptide de novo architectures to small-molecule MS/MS in a competition setting.

## Key Question 3 — NMR-to-structure and IR-to-structure elucidation challenges/benchmarks and LLM structure-elucidation benchmarks (2022–2026): tasks, success rates, what transfers

### Takeaway
Spectrum→SMILES transformers now reach 44–64% top-1 from IR and 73% top-1 from 1H+13C NMR on simulated data (up to 96% with multimodal fine-tuning on experimental spectra), and formula-conditioned generation is the standard trick; on the same simulated benchmark MS/MS is the weakest modality (≤23.5% top-1). LLM-centric benchmarks (MolPuzzle 2024, SpectrumBench 2025, MolQuest 2026) and two 2026 MS/MS-specific studies show off-the-shelf LLMs achieve ~0% exact match de novo, contradict their own reasoning 73–92% of the time, and succeed only by recall on well-documented compounds (52% vs 2% on undocumented) — but they are useful as *rerankers* when fed candidates from a de novo generator (which lifts undocumented-compound accuracy to 38%).

### Cited Findings

#### Human/educational challenge sites
- NMR-Challenge.com (IOCB Prague; J. Chem. Educ. 2023, DOI 10.1021/acs.jchemed.2c01067): >160 structure-from-NMR assignments from real samples, basic (1D 1H/13C) and advanced (2D) levels, drawing tool with immediate feedback; continuously updated — [Charles University / IOCB announcement](https://orgchem.natur.cuni.cz/node/4971); later described as >500 real NMR spectra of 200 organic compounds — [RSC Education](https://edu.rsc.org/education-research/improve-your-learners-nmr-interpretation-skills/4019674.article)

#### Spectra-to-SMILES transformers (IBM Research and others)
- Alberts, Laino, Vaucher, "Leveraging infrared spectroscopy for automated structure elucitation" (Communications Chemistry 7:268, 2024-11-16): pretrain on 634,585 simulated IR spectra, fine-tune on 3,453 experimental; top-1 44.4%, top-10 69.8% (6–13 heavy atoms); scaffold-only top-1 84.5%, top-10 93.0% (preprint had 45.33%/78.5%) — [PMC11569215](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11569215)
- Alberts, Zipoli, Laino, "Setting new benchmarks in AI-driven infrared structure elucidation" (Digital Discovery, June 2025): top-1 63.79%, top-10 83.95% vs prior SOTA 53.56%/80.36% — [RSC Digital Discovery](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2025/dd/d5dd00131e)

- Hu et al., "Accurate and Efficient Structure Elucidation from Routine One-Dimensional NMR Spectra Using Multitask Machine Learning" (ACS Central Science 10(11):2162–2170, Nov 2024; Stanford/NYU): transformer assembling fragments + CNN spectral encoder; molecules ≤19 heavy atoms; without formula, correct molecule in top-15 in 69.6% of cases ("up to 11 orders of magnitude" search-space reduction) — [PMC11613330](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11613330/)
- ChefNMR, "Atomic Diffusion Models for Small Molecule Structure Elucidation from NMR Spectra" (Xiong … Seyedsayamdost, Zhong; NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2512.03127, 2025-12-02): formula-conditioned atomic diffusion with a non-equivariant transformer; simulated 1D NMR for >111,000 natural products; "accuracy of over 65%" on challenging natural products — [arXiv 2512.03127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03127)

- SpectrumWorld / SpectrumBench (Yang et al.; arXiv 2508.01188, Aug 2025, v4 Sep 2025): 14 tasks in 4 levels (signal, perception, semantic, generation) over >10 spectrum types incl. IR, NMR, MS, Raman, HSQC, UV-Vis; 572 QA items; 23 multimodal LLMs; best overall Gemini-2.5-Pro 67.81%, best open InternVL3.5-241B 65.50%; structure-elucidation (SE) task average 56.08% (InternS1-think 90.0%, Claude-3.5-Sonnet 82.5%, Claude-4-Sonnet 16.25%); generation-level tasks very low (inverse problems 3.50%, de novo generation 6.41%); generation scored by GPT-4o as judge — [arXiv 2508.01188v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01188v4)
- MolQuest (Han et al.; arXiv 2603.25253, 2026-03-26): multi-turn agentic elucidation on authentic experimental data (plan experiments, integrate NMR and MS, refine hypotheses); SOTA model ≈50% accuracy, most models <30% — [arXiv 2603.25253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25253)
- Wang et al., "How well can off-the-shelf LLMs elucidate molecular structures from mass spectra using chain-of-thought reasoning?" (arXiv 2601.06289, 2026-01-09): zero-shot CoT on MassSpecGym (231k spectra, ~29k structures; MCES ≥10 split) with formula, adduct, instrument and CE given; Claude-3.5-Sonnet SMILES validity 93.70% but formula consistency 0.00%, exact match 0/0 (top-1/top-10), Tanimoto top-1 0.09; GPT-4o-mini validity 75.41%, exact 0; Llama-3-70B validity 38.12%; DBE correctness 3.9–4.2%; reasoning–answer contradiction rate 72.8% (Claude) to 92.2% (GPT-4o-mini); specialist baseline DiffMS 2.30%/4.25% exact, Tanimoto 0.28/0.39; conclusion: CoT is "a stylistic scaffold" and chemically constrained decoding + spectral encoders are needed — [arXiv 2601.06289v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06289v1)

### Inferences
- Assessment — IBM IR/NMR transformers and multimodal dataset: (1) Domain Medium (small molecules; MS/MS included only as simulated); (2) Core challenge High (inverse spectrum→SMILES with formula prior, top-k evaluation); (3) Usefulness High: establishes formula-conditioned seq2seq/diffusion as the baseline recipe, quantifies that MS/MS is a far weaker single modality than NMR (23.5% vs 73% top-1 under identical settings), and shows "simulated pretrain → experimental fine-tune" as the path around data scarcity — the natural-product gap they flag is exactly the 2026 test distribution.
- Assessment — Hu et al. / ChefNMR / NMRGym / DiffSpectra: (1) Medium; (2) High; (3) Medium–High: three design choices recur — explicit formula conditioning, fragment-assembly or atom-level generation that keeps atom counts valid, and scaffold-split evaluation to block leakage; ChefNMR shows a natural-product-specific training corpus (111k NPs) is feasible from COCONUT-like sources.
- Assessment — MolPuzzle / SpectrumBench / MolQuest / K-MSE: (1) Medium; (2) Medium (QA-style; small test sets; LLM-judged generation); (3) Medium: they tell you *not* to rely on general LLMs for the de novo slot, but K-MSE's "knowledge base + spectrum-structure scorer as reward" is a reasonable blueprint for a candidate-expansion + reranking agent.
- Assessment — the two 2026 MS/MS LLM studies: (1) High (MassSpecGym MS/MS); (2) High (novelty split, de novo vs rerank); (3) High: they operationalize the contest's hidden novelty classes — LLM recall covers class (1)/(2) molecules "where the literature is thick", while class (3) needs a specialist generator; the practical architecture suggested is generator-proposes/LLM-or-scorer-reranks, with formula consistency enforced mechanically (LLMs score 0% on it).
- Canonicalization lesson: MolPuzzle's 1.4% exact match and the CoT study's 0% exact match were computed on strings; the 2026 metric's tautomer-canonical InChIKey14 means teams must canonicalize every generated SMILES (RDKit tautomer enumeration + InChIKey14) and de-duplicate across the 25 slots, otherwise duplicates waste ranks.

### Gaps
- NMRGym and MolQuest abstracts give no baseline numbers; DiffSpectra's dataset (likely QM9-derived simulated spectra) is unverified from the model card.
- IBM's 96% top-1 claim is from a workshop abstract without test-set details.
- The ICBINB poster does not name the LLM or de novo generator used; results are from the abstract only.

## Key Question 4 — Governance analogs (CASP, CAFA, CAGI, CAMI, SAMPL/CACHE) and community benchmarking guidance: hidden/novel targets, blind evaluation, metric design (brief)

### Takeaway
The CASP-family experiments converge on a few transferable rules — ground truth that did not exist publicly before the deadline (newly solved structures, newly annotated proteins, newly sequenced genomes, unpublished assays), explicit difficulty/novelty tiers (CASP TBM vs FM; CAFA no-knowledge vs limited-knowledge; CAMI strain-madness), multiple complementary metrics with bootstrap CIs, naive/BLAST-style baselines to expose shortcut methods, and independent assessors — and the June 2026 "MassSpecGym in the Wild" audit shows why: 17 of 26 MS/MS papers had leakage, shortcut or metric bugs, with inflations as large as 82%→7% Hit@1 from a canonicalization artifact and ~30× from formula leakage.

### Cited Findings
- CASP15 (2022): ~100 groups, >53,000 models, 127 targets, 5 categories; TBM and FM single-chain categories were merged for assessment but evaluation units were still labeled TBM-easy/TBM-hard/FM/TBM-FM, with >1/3 of EUs in the historically hardest FM class; 132 groups on 112 EUs from 77 single-chain targets; most top groups used AlphaFold2 in some way — [Prediction Center CASP15](https://predictioncenter.org/casp15/); [target-processing paper (PMC10792517)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10792517); [single-chain assessment (PMC10687315)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10687315)
- CAFA3 (Zhou et al., Genome Biology 20:244, 2019-11-19): targets are proteins lacking experimental annotation at prediction time; benchmarks are those that *gain* experimental annotations after the deadline; "no-knowledge" vs "limited-knowledge" benchmark types and full vs partial evaluation modes; metrics Fmax (with bootstrap 95% CIs), Smin, weighted Fmax; Naïve and BLAST baselines have not improved since CAFA2 and "many methods rely heavily" on them; CAFA2→3 gains smaller than CAFA1→2 — [PMC6864930](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6864930/); NK/LK definitions in supplementary README — [CAFA3 supplementary](https://calla.rnet.missouri.edu/rnaminer/transfun_all_data/data/supplementary_data/00README.txt)
- CAGI retrospective (Genome Biology 2024): 5 editions, 50 challenges, 738 submissions; predictions on unpublished phenotypes scored by independent assessors; multiple metrics (R², Pearson, Kendall; ROC AUC and truncated AUC at FPR 0–0.2; MCC; clinical likelihood ratios); negative control (PolyPhen-2) and positive control (experimental-uncertainty upper bound); 95% CIs; prospective "Annotate All Missense" to avoid training-set contamination; data quality as the main obstacle; watch for circularity and participants "exploiting unintended information" — [PMC10882881](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10882881/)
- CAMI I (Nature Methods 2017): benchmark built from ~700 newly sequenced microorganisms and ~600 novel viruses/plasmids; methods degraded with related strains and below family rank — [UEA repository](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/87984)
- CAMI II (Nature Methods 2022): 1,680 genomes + 599 circular elements, 772 newly sequenced; "strain-madness" set (408 new genomes, 395 with close relatives); 332 teams registered, 30 submitted 5,002 results from 76 program versions; executable software + parameters requested for reproducibility; metric choice changes rankings (filtering smallest 1% of bins raised purity >71% but cut completeness to ~24%), hence ranking across several metrics; inclusion of public genomes inflated results vs CAMI I (Kraken species-rank completeness 84.6% vs 50%) — [PMC9007738](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9007738/)
- "Ten Simple Rules for a Community Computational Challenge" (Friedberg, Wass, Mooney, Radivojac; PLoS Comput Biol 2015-04-23): separate organizers/data providers/assessors; publish metrics and evaluation code early; keep rules fixed within a round; credit predictors — [PMC4408123](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4408123/)
- de Jonge et al., "Good practices and recommendations for using and benchmarking computational metabolomics metabolite annotation tools" (Metabolomics 18:103, 2022-12-05): stratify test sets by class/mass/instrument; 100-spectrum test sets "vary widely"; library-inherited bias inflates analogue-search results; pre-cleaned library spectra overestimate ML performance; top-1 primary with top-n complementary; report recall–accuracy trade-offs; SIRIUS performance drops above ~500 Da; recommends blind community challenges such as CASMI — [PMC9722809](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9722809/)
- "MassSpecGym in the Wild: Uncovering and Correcting Evaluation Pitfalls in AI-Driven Molecule Discovery" (Liu, Bushuiev … Healey, Pluskal, Coley; arXiv 2606.19624, 2026-06-17; ICML 2026): issues in ≥17 of 26 papers. Leakage: decoders pretrained with only exact-InChIKey exclusion (accuracy rises with Tanimoto ≥0.7 to pretraining molecules); synthetic-spectrum leakage via ICEBERG (R@1 67.75% when simulator saw test molecules vs 53.76% leak-free vs 9.57% no augmentation); formula leakage (filtering random candidates to the true formula lifts Hit@1 0.43%→12.98%; ground-truth formula nearly doubles MIST retrieval; MIST-CF formulas 26.64% clean vs 35.15% contaminated). Shortcuts: ground-truth SMILES formatting differed from RDKit-canonical decoys — Hit@1 82.41%→6.70% (DreaMS+ChemBERTa), 63.89%→7.60%, 99.66%→8.2% after correction; spectrum-blind PubChem default order gives 49.98% Hit@1 and "number of chiral atoms" 4.88%. Bugs: MIST padding-mask bug; corrected MIST+MolForge top-1 10.73%→31.75%. Metric divergence: use 14-char InChIKey, 2048-bit ECFP4, pinned MCES (threshold 15), 99.9% bootstrap CIs over 20,000 resamples; v1.5 release with analog-excluded pretraining sets (2.5M/50M molecules), data-safe MIST-CF/ICEBERG/DreaMS; candidate-pool bias remains open — [arXiv 2606.19624v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19624v1); [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/70824)
- SAMPL blind physical-property challenges: SAMPL5 logD (53 molecules; 76 submissions, 18 groups) found groups "struggled to estimate their own model error"; SAMPL6 pKa found many tools right on macroscopic pKa but wrong on microscopic protonation sites; SAMPL7 (22 N-acylsulfonamides; 33 logP, 9 pKa submissions) — [SAMPL5 (PMC5209301)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5209301); [SAMPL7 (JCAMD 2021)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10822-021-00397-3)
- CACHE prospective hit-finding: #1 LRRK2 WDR — 1,955 compounds, 73 SPR binders <150 μM, 7 confirmed series; #5 MCHR1 — 24 teams, 1,455 compounds, 9 groups with convincing ligands; paired rounds to limit false positives/negatives — [CACHE #1 bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.18.603797.full.pdf); [CACHE #5 results](https://cache-challenge.org/node/3000)

### Inferences
- Novelty tiers are the norm: CASP (TBM/FM), CAFA (NK/LK), CAMI (new genomes/strain-madness) each pre-declare difficulty classes and report per-class results; the 2026 hidden classes (library / in-PubChem / de novo) follow this pattern, so local validation should be stratified the same way and expect the FM-like class to dominate the error budget.
- Shortcut exposure: CAFA's finding that many methods lean on Naïve/BLAST and CAGI's negative controls argue for running spectrum-blind baselines (precursor-mass-only, PubChem-frequency-only, formula-only) against your own pipeline; MassSpecGym-in-the-Wild shows such baselines can reach ~50% Hit@1 on biased candidate pools, so a method that cannot beat them is exploiting priors not spectra.
- Leakage hygiene for 2026 specifically: exclude test-like analogs (Tanimoto ≥0.7 / MCES <10) when estimating de novo generalization; never let a spectrum simulator or pretraining corpus see held-out structures; canonicalize queries and candidates identically (tautomer-canonical InChIKey14 end-to-end); pin metric implementations and report bootstrap CIs.
- Metric sensitivity: CAMI II's rank reversals across metrics and CAGI's multi-metric policy suggest tracking Hit@1, MRR@25 and per-class MRR separately — MRR@25 rewards filling all 25 slots with distinct canonical candidates.

### Gaps
- CAFA3 Methods section (exact NK/LK definitions, annotation window) was truncated in the fetched text; definitions were taken from the supplementary README.
- CASP's current handling of "novel" targets after AF2 (post-CASP15 rounds) was not researched; the brief above is CASP15-level.

### Inferences
- Assessment — MS2Mol/EnvedaDark: (1) High (NP MS/MS, Enveda instruments); (2) High (de novo under novelty; similarity-threshold metrics; confidence gating); (3) Medium–High: the 21%/62% figures are the only public prior on how a strong de novo model performs on Enveda-style "dark" natural products, and the confidence scorer (98% close-match on the top decile) is a template for deciding *which* molecules deserve de novo slots vs database slots in the 25-rank list.
- Assessment — Enveda-180: (1) High (timsTOF, same acquisition family as the test); (2) Medium (synthetic drug-like Enamine molecules, not natural products); (3) High for instrument-shift calibration: it is the only large in-instrument library, so it is the natural source for timsTOF-specific intensity/fragmentation statistics, adduct priors, and for validating spectrum simulators or domain-adaptation of models trained on Orbitrap-heavy public libraries.
- Assessment — TIMS-Bench: (1) High; (2) Low–Medium (tool/feature benchmarking, not structure ID); (3) Medium: supplies curated timsTOF ground-truth sets (NIST SRM, plant spike-ins, ReFRAME with CCS) that can serve as extra identity-disjoint validation spectra with CCS values — relevant if ion-mobility is exposed in the test data.
- Follow-up expectations: the "persistent benchmark" language plus Enveda's involvement in the MassSpecGym v1.5 audit suggest the hidden test set may be re-used for post-contest leaderboards; no CASMI 2027 or recurring series is announced anywhere found.
